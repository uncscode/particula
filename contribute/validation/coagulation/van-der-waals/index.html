      Van der Waals and dielectric coagulation validation

      The CPU strategy and direct and prepared CUDA solvers evaluate a complete thermal encounter kernel for homogeneous spheres in air. The strongest experimental agreement in this comparison is for unequal neutral salt particles. The equal-size acid measurements show a larger size-dependent residual. An independent trajectory calculation found a 13.8% underestimate for a strongly attractive opposite-charge pair in the transition regime. This result sets a boundary on the supported charge combinations.

      Model and operating range

      Section titled “Model and operating range”

      Supply a nonnegative effective Hamaker constant in joules and a common relative permittivity . Each pair requires radius ratio at most 32, , and , where and . Opposite-sign pairs additionally require . The implementation raises a domain error outside these limits. They bound the implemented calculation. Physical evidence within this range comes from the individual cases below. Material constants are fixed throughout a run, including after a merger.

      • The CPU supports discrete bins, radius PDFs and particle-resolved populations. Bin populations retain their prescribed charge. Resolved particles carry signed charge and merge each species separately.
      • Native CUDA supports equal positive counts within each box of a particle-resolved population. Admission and sampling use the same actual pair law, including its sum with settling or turbulent shear. Preparation transfers the material response table once. Restart checks its version and material inputs. The CPU resolved path allocates a dense pair matrix.
      • Setting removes dispersion. With unit permittivity and zero charges the control is the dimensionless hard-sphere transition law. Its continuum and ballistic limits follow analytically. The dimensionless interpolation differs from the Fuchs interpolation in the ordinary Brownian strategy.

      The theory page defines the potential, transport limits and assumptions. Sceats gives an alternative all-regime treatment. Near-contact hydrodynamic resistance, retardation, molecular-scale structure and composition-dependent optical properties would change the interpretation of an inferred Hamaker constant. Sceats (1989) · Alam (1987)

      Numerical verification

      Section titled “Numerical verification”

      The 44 material and geometry cases sample radius ratios 1, 3.17, 5 and 31.9. Controls include hard spheres, pure Coulomb capture, a charged sphere on either side of a neutral partner, repulsive barriers, and weak and strong dispersion. Analytic checks also cover the dipole and dispersion far-field powers, charge-sign reversal and pair exchange.

      Quantity Reference and result
      Continuum enhancement Adaptive diffusion-resistance integral. Maximum relative difference across 44 CPU cases was 0.056%.
      Ballistic enhancement Adaptive search for critical impact parameters followed by Maxwell-flux quadrature. Maximum relative difference was 0.267%.
      Reference convergence Six equal or highly unequal charged pairs were refined from multipole order 160 to 224 and 256 to 512 radial intervals, with tighter orbital quadrature. The largest change in either enhancement was 0.024%. A quadrature roundoff warning occurred at the tightest setting. These solvers share the electrostatic boundary equations.
      Dimensional CUDA kernel All 132 comparisons at diffusive Knudsen numbers passed. Maximum relative error for rates above of hard spheres was 0.029% (criterion 1%). Below that threshold, maximum absolute error divided by the hard-sphere rate was (criterion ).

      Independent transition-regime trajectories

      Section titled “Independent transition-regime trajectories”

      A relative-coordinate Langevin solver followed inward Maxwellian flux from a finite outer sphere to either contact or escape. An exterior diffusive resistance accounts for supply to the outer boundary. The solver uses the force profile directly and estimates absorption from hit counts. The campaign used 524,288 launches per case at three bath-radius and timestep settings, 20,447,232 trajectories in total. Zero trajectories reached the time cutoff. Binomial sampling uncertainty is conditional on the chosen force and boundary model. Varying outer radius from 5 to 8 contact distances and halving the step scale tests sensitivity to the numerical boundary and step.

      Cai et al. provide five tabulated NaCl rates for 3.6–10 nm particles colliding with a 100 nm partner. Applying the same neutral salt scenario at 298 K and 1 atm gives absolute rates 37–44% below the measured values. Particula predicts enhancements of 1.21–1.31 relative to its hard-sphere law. Using the paper’s tabulated Knudsen numbers and fitted limiting factors in Eqs. S7–S14 gives 1.44–1.51 with the Alam interpolation and 1.50–1.56 with Sceats. Those calculations use different limiting factors and hard-sphere baselines. This disagreement matters when using the mechanism for a sub-10 nm coagulation sink.       Reproduction

      Section titled “Reproduction”
