      Native gas chemistry qualification

      A single native mechanism drives NumPy and resident CUDA integration for closed, irreversible gas reactions at prescribed temperature. Three synthetic experiments isolate different errors: assigning the wrong storage column, confusing mass with molar concentration, and losing precision in the stiff solver. The numerical implementation was qualified at revision 4825b85fbd with Python 3.12.12, NumPy 2.3.4, SciPy 1.16.2 and Warp 1.11.1 on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5060. Mechanism guide

      First-order conversion and external mass

      Section titled “First-order conversion and external mass”

      For closed A → B with equal molar masses and s⁻¹, A follows and B gains the missing A mass. The regression checks both species, inert gas, and extensive mass in boxes of 1 and 2 m³. In the worked example an independent pulse adds A at 4 s and ventilation dilutes all gases at 0.05 s⁻¹. The pre-pulse reading is at 4 s. The first post-pulse reading is at 4.5 s. The full 41-point trajectories used 0.25 s process intervals and were checked against analytical concentrations with rtol=2e-9, atol=1e-20 kg/m³ and A+B inventories with rtol=1e-12, atol=1e-22 kg.

      Backend Maximum absolute A or B error (kg/m³) Added mass in boxes 1 and 2 (kg)
      NumPy 1.7272 × 10⁻²⁰ 3 × 10⁻⁹, 2 × 10⁻⁹
      CUDA 1.7261 × 10⁻²⁰ 3 × 10⁻⁹, 2 × 10⁻⁹

      Restarted and uninterrupted histories and source ledgers matched bit for bit within each backend. Each run has a prescribed intervention at 4 s. The experiment snapshots the pre-pulse state, applies the external dose on the host, and begins the next segment at 4 s. The restart variants resume the pre-pulse checkpoint and another checkpoint at 4.5 s. Rate evaluations, trial decisions and linear solves stay on the selected backend. Executed example and figure

      Bimolecular reaction and binding

      Section titled “Bimolecular reaction and binding”

      A + B → C uses molar masses 0.01, 0.02 and 0.03 kg/mol. Initially A and B have equal molar concentrations (1 and 2 mol/m³ in the respective boxes). At 298 and 318 K the rate coefficient is m³/mol/s. The two reactants follow , and the product gains one mole for each mole of A lost. RK23 and ROS3 match the analytical concentrations with relative tolerance 10⁻⁷. The inert gas retains its exact value. These tests run on NumPy, Warp CPU and CUDA. Loading JSON and YAML into permuted gas layouts and rebinding produces exactly matching physical concentrations. Stale layouts, incompatible masses, duplicate bindings and failed elemental balances have dedicated rejection tests.

      Stiffness and full-precision coefficients

      Section titled “Stiffness and full-precision coefficients”

      For the Robertson test use normalized initial state (1, 0, 0), concentration scale 1 mol/m³ and time scale 1 s. Declare equal 0.1 kg/mol molar masses for the three synthetic species. The test integrates from zero independently to 10⁻⁴, 10⁻², 1 and 100 s. The reference uses the Stage 2A right-hand side with independent Radau and BDF integrations; both agree within rtol=1e-8, atol=1e-14. The production solution must agree with each reference at tight settings within rtol=2e-6, atol=1e-12, with a separate comparison for every species. Robertson benchmark · KPP ROS3 method

      Backend Relative tolerance Maximum absolute errors in A, B, C (mol/m³) Accepted / rejected Factorizations / solves
      NumPy 10⁻⁵ 5.84 × 10⁻⁹, 1.17 × 10⁻¹¹, 5.84 × 10⁻⁹ 1064 / 11 1075 / 9675
      CUDA 10⁻⁵ 5.84 × 10⁻⁹, 1.17 × 10⁻¹¹, 5.84 × 10⁻⁹ 1064 / 11 1075 / 9675
      NumPy 10⁻⁸ 2.40 × 10⁻¹³, 8.04 × 10⁻¹⁵, 2.43 × 10⁻¹³ 13582 / 11 13593 / 122337
      CUDA 10⁻⁸ 2.35 × 10⁻¹³, 8.04 × 10⁻¹⁵, 2.47 × 10⁻¹³ 13582 / 11 13593 / 122337

      The work counts sum the four independent integrations for the first box. Absolute tolerance is 10⁻¹⁴ mol/m³. Every accepted endpoint is nonnegative and normalized total concentration is conserved to relative tolerance 10⁻¹² in this equal-mass case. Tightening the tolerance reduced error in all three species. Using full-precision device tableau constants matters: intermediate rounding of those constants to float32 produced an error floor near 8 × 10⁻⁹ mol/m³ for A and C during development. A dedicated device regression checks the refined solution. This study establishes no GPU speedup or large-network scaling claim.

      NCAR gas mechanism import

      Section titled “NCAR gas mechanism import”

      The converter tests an A → C exponential and an A + B → C bimolecular trajectory at 298 and 318 K. The source documents use nonzero temperature exponents, per-molecule activation energy or its kelvin equivalent, and a different species order from the simulation. Rates from the imported and independently authored native mechanisms meet the 10⁻¹² relative bound. Both boxes meet the analytical trajectory and inventory checks on NumPy, Warp CPU and an RTX 5060. Fifteen conversion cases passed. A known imbalance in reacting molecular weights rejected before export even when an inert weight was absent. The rejection preserved the existing destination file. The pinned NCAR full example also rejected its unsupported emissions section.

      Warp compatibility

      Section titled “Warp compatibility”

      The ROS3 back substitution uses an explicit read, divide and store for its three-dimensional work array. Warp 1.19.0 rejected the earlier in-place division during kernel compilation. All 32 chemistry and chamber tests passed with Warp 1.11.1 and again with 1.19.0 on the RTX 5060 host, including the Warp CPU and CUDA cases.

      The sparse resident update at revision b90bab07a9 reproduced all four Robertson refinement rows and both chamber errors reported above. Stiff ensemble timing and workspace
