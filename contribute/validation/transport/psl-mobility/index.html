      Dry PSL mobility sizing against 49 measured slip factors

      Kim et al. (2005) reported electrical-mobility measurements on dry polystyrene latex spheres. This independent comparison uses all 49 numerical rows from Tables 7a–c on printed pages 42–43. The guide gives the experimental interpretation. The CPU example and downloadable bundle include the observations in their original table order. Original NIST paper

      Published mobility and inferred diameter

      Section titled “Published mobility and inferred diameter”

      The CSV records pressure in kPa, temperature in K, sheath and aerosol flows in L/min, peak voltage in V, and the dimensionless Kn, slip factor C and slip parameter A. The source identifiers are the printed table and one-based row. The diameter assignments and charge magnitudes are 100.7 nm and one elementary charge for Table 7a, 269.0 nm and two for Table 7b, and 19.90 nm and one for Table 7c. The source PDF used for extraction had SHA-256 5a44701000cb8ec2252461829c403e0c17cf6c890b6af93d00a281aa862ad696. Kim et al., Tables 7a–c

      For each row we reconstruct the electrical mobility magnitude as . Use the paper’s viscosity and set the mean free path to . With the gas state fixed, solve for the diameter that gives the same mobility with Particula’s slip law. The full study used Brent and bisection on a 1–2,000 nm bracket. The maximum difference between the roots was below 7 × 10⁻¹¹ nm, and the largest forward mobility round-trip error was below 2 × 10⁻¹⁰% relative. Relative slip differences use the measured C as denominator.

      Two pressure entries change the 269 nm comparison

      Section titled “Two pressure entries change the 269 nm comparison”

      The final two rows of Table 7b print 84.26 kPa and Kn = 6.014 for 269 nm spheres. The paper’s Eq. 11 at the printed conditions gives Kn ≈ 0.602, about a factor of ten smaller. The first of those rows also lists A = 1.436, while (C − 1)/Kn = 1.576. We checked the printed PDF, retained its values and used the tabulated Kn and C in the primary comparison. Both rows are flagged in the downloadable results and excluded from all pressure-derived summaries. The remaining 47 rows reproduce tabulated Kn to within 0.14% from pressure, temperature and size. Dropping the two flagged rows moves the overall mean slip difference from +1.01% to +1.03% and the 269 nm mean from +1.38% to +2.15% (two near-atmospheric measurements). The actual pressure of the flagged measurements remains unresolved. Table 7b and Eq. 11

      The example records a null diameter in the native-gas calculation for a row whose pressure-derived mean free path differs from its Kn-derived value by more than 1%. Both rows remain in the matched-gas calculation. At 19.90 nm the diameter assignment depends on the authors’ mobility calibration. Repeated pressure settings and shared calibration errors limit the interpretation of a pooled mean.

      Reproduce the calibration transfer

      Section titled “Reproduce the calibration transfer”

      The synthetic scan uses the published fit at 296.15 K and 101.3 kPa. A multiplier of 0.98237 on the Particula mobility scale matches the synthetic reference at 100 nm. All other sizes test transfer. The saved plot data contain both the per-row inversions and the synthetic scan.

      The study used Particula source 0.3.2 at revision 2303121c905aabdd99da6ec7e398bba89633891c on CPU, Python 3.12.12, NumPy 2.3.4, SciPy 1.16.2 and Matplotlib 3.10.7. From a checkout with the Particula environment active, use the following command.

      From the extracted example bundle run python example.py. The two near-atmospheric 100.7 nm rows should yield 102.02 and 102.03 nm. The figure and row-level numerical results are also saved on the example page.
