# What the camera observes and how the droplets evolve

The central comparison asks how many suspended water droplets of each wet diameter a hologram would have counted as the cloud evolved. Two pieces of information enter at different times. The first visible droplet spectrum sets the initial population. The authors’ temperature and vapor measurements then drive the gas conditions during each expansion. Using those later **gas** measurements still leaves the later **droplet** observations available to test the forward calculation. [Experiment, Figures 6–7](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0344133) · [Digitized inputs](data.md)

## From an early spectrum to individual droplets

We assigned the visible 2.5–3 s distribution to its midpoint, 2.75 s. A stratified draw across wet diameters supplied 768 water droplets in each of 32 independent realizations per expansion. Each realization’s volume was chosen to reproduce the observed concentration. Particula’s finite-vapor condensation calculation transfers water between the shared gas and the droplets, including Kelvin curvature and the resistance from latent heat. The laser constrains vapor every 50 ms. The forward model uses mean temperatures and approximate post-expansion pressures of 668 hPa with fans and 637 hPa without. These are conditional predictions under observed gas forcing. [Particula condensation](../../../guide/processes/condensation.md) · [Source and replay commands](data.md)

Brownian motion, differential settling and velocity differences due to turbulent shear supply the collision kernel. A merger removes one droplet and conserves combined liquid mass. Wall deposition removes the deposited droplet and its water. The forward case recalculates the removal probability from each droplet’s current radius using Particula’s rectangular-wall model. Its 0.508 m square footprint and 0.495 m height give the same sidewall and floor areas per unit volume as the idealized cylindrical body. The near-wall eddy parameter (3.6 s⁻¹), unit capture for differential settling and fan-dependent dissipation are scenario inputs. Deposition includes settling in this well-mixed calculation. ## From a chamber population to an optical spectrum

The spatial model divides the cylinder into communicating volumes. It advances finite vapor, condensation, collisions, size-dependent settling and deposition. Air motion transports both droplets and vapor. The spatial calculation treats deposition at the side and ends separately from settling to the floor. To predict the visible spectrum, integrate each size over the small prism sampled by the beam. The reconstruction depth increases with droplet diameter. The thermocouples sample specific locations, while vapor is constrained on a separate path. Local humidity, circulation and the early radial population gradient are only partially constrained. [Geometry and sensitivity](camera.md)

Fitting starts with the 3–5 s spatial background. The sectional calculation tracks the number at each size in each cell. After condensation or evaporation, it allocates droplets to adjacent sizes using their liquid volumes (proportional to diameter cubed). For example, a 12 µm drop contributes about 54.2% of a drop to the 11 µm bin and 45.8% to the 13 µm bin, preserving both one drop and its water content. Below the smallest bin, a shrinking drop can evaporate into a zero-volume state. At the largest bin further growth is capped and the unused vapor stays in the gas. Across the fitted sectional runs, the largest cell-level imbalance between vapor lost and liquid gained (or vice versa) was below 9 × 10⁻¹⁹ kg/m³ per condensation step. We measure that balance before collisions, settling, wall deposition and exchange between cells. Gradients obtained by automatic differentiation through that calculation let the optimizer adjust effective collision rates, two transport scales and a bounded temperature contrast. The whole-spectrum objective compares absolute concentrations in 68 one-micrometre classes, including empty bins. A regularization term discourages abrupt changes in time. One collision multiplier applies to pairs with both parents in 20–48 µm; another applies to other pairs. The main fitted histories were then replayed with six ensembles of approximately 65,536 individually evolving droplets and 576 chamber volumes. The stochastic merger sampler uses Particula’s physical pair kernels and conserves the liquid water of each merging pair. The sequential spatial update is a sectional calculation that conserves chamber inventory in every size bin.

## Which comparisons use withheld observations?

The **early fit** uses optical spectra only before 5 s. The **blocked fit** uses 3–5 and 5.5–6.25 s. Its 5–5.5 s comparison interpolates across a gap with fitting observations on both sides. After 6.25 s, the fitted controls are frozen and the model advances under the measured gas forcing. The spatial update uses earlier camera residuals only in training quarters. The four-family ranking first scores paired model configurations under both fan settings and both withheld windows, then selects the lowest whole-spectrum density error in each family. These are two previously studied experimental records. The scores describe transfer within the available records. [Recalculate the fan-off result](burst.py) · [Full-study figures](index.md)

The virtual cameras sample the same simulation at each time step. Their position changes the sampling weights and leaves the simulated droplets alone. One source intervention moves existing large droplets to a parcel above the beam, conserving each size’s chamber inventory. The other increases the likelihood of middle-sized droplets merging near the original beam. Their central excess peaks were calibrated to a common 0.080 droplets/cm³ during the measured peak quarter.
