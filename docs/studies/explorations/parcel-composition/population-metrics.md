# Which population metrics tell us where aerosol species reside?

Two parcels can agree on χ, every species inventory, and the full dry size spectrum while disagreeing on which particles activate. The first quantity I would add to χ is **how much of the chemical variation is explained by dry size**. A second, complementary output is a size-resolved table of particle compositions. The first can track a six-hour simulation in one line. The second lets another scientist inspect the chemistry or calculate a response. The choice of species, weighting, and size resolution matters for both. [Six-hour exchange and Pallas comparison](exchange.md)

## An information budget for the Riemer–West index

Imagine selecting a tiny element of dry mass uniformly from a parcel. Record its chemical species S, its host particle J, and that particle’s dry-size bin B. The bulk species entropy is H(S). Once the host particle is known, the remaining uncertainty is H(S | J), the mass-weighted mean of the individual particle entropies. These are precisely the two entropies used to calculate χ. Their difference is the information particle identity provides about species. Because each particle belongs to one size bin, the information chain rule gives

$$
H(S)-H(S\mid J) = I(S;B) + I(S;J\mid B).
$$

The first term measures how much knowing dry size tells us about species. The second measures the additional information in particle identity among particles of similar size. Both are computable from the original species masses. For a weighted simulation, multiply particle masses by represented counts when accumulating inventories and entropies. Evaluate the entropy of each particle’s composition before combining distinct particles. Using base-two logarithms gives bits; χ is recovered from the two entropies using powers of two. The additive split applies to the entropy difference, while χ itself is a ratio of transformed entropies. This is an application of an existing identity. [Riemer and West (2013)](https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-13-11423-2013) · [Cover and Thomas, information chain rules](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/liam/teaching/neurostat-spr11/papers/EM/Cover%26Thomas-Ch2.pdf)

| Dry-mass-weighted description, either parcel | Hour 0 | Hour 3 | Hour 6 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| χ | 0.3841 | 0.3841 | 0.3841 |
| Total information about species from knowing the particle (bits) | 0.5310 | 0.5310 | 0.5310 |
| Explained by 12 dry-size intervals (bits) | 0.2210 | 0.0185 | 0.0017 |
| Remaining within those intervals (bits) | 0.3100 | 0.5125 | 0.5293 |

At hour zero the 12 size intervals explain **41.6%** of the available information about species. Six hours later they explain **0.31%**. Parcel exchange redistributes the same two particle types across sizes while leaving every particle’s composition intact. The result is a shift of chemical variation from *between* sizes to *within* sizes. Use the absolute terms when the total approaches zero, since the percentage then becomes unstable. The two parcels have equal information budgets even when one carries more salt in the middle size mode. A plot of the salt-rich number fraction against diameter supplies the missing direction. This decomposition is exact for the declared bins; finer nested bins can move information from the second term to the first.

## What else can one learn about a population?

Start with the joint empirical distribution of dry diameter and the particle’s vector of species fractions. For a number-based question give particle i weight proportional to its represented count. For dry mass use count times dry mass. Preserve total number and species inventories separately so two parcels with equal normalized distributions but different loadings remain distinguishable. Surface-area weighting or an optical cross-section can be appropriate for a specific observable. Declare those choices, dry versus wet size, and mass versus volume fractions before comparing results.

| Question | Candidate from another field | Aerosol interpretation and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How sensitive is diversity to rare species? | Hill diversity profile with orders 0, 1, and 2 | Richness counts detected species, exponential Shannon entropy weights them by abundance, and inverse Simpson diversity emphasizes common species. Declare whether categories are chemical species within a particle or particle types within the parcel. Relabeling chemically distinct species can preserve the whole profile. [Hill (1973)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1934352) |
| Which species share particles? | Joint occurrence probabilities and pairwise composition moments | Within each size interval, report a conditional fraction such as the share of black-carbon-containing particles that also contain sulfate, with detection limits and denominators. For continuous fractions, report the weighted mean product. Both can distinguish paired species that χ conflates. Pairwise summaries can still miss associations among three or more species. |
| Do chemical differences matter for a process? | Rao’s quadratic entropy | Supply a physical dissimilarity between classes and calculate the expected dissimilarity of two independent draws. Distances based on κ and distances based on absorptivity ask different questions. Record the distance and the unit of sampling. A single dispersion value loses the locations of the classes. [Rao (1982)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0040580982900041) |
| Which species trade off within a particle? | Aitchison log-ratios and their variation matrix | The variance of log(sulfate/organic mass) measures variation in a ratio independent of total particle mass. Ordinary fraction correlations can arise from the sum-to-one constraint. True absences make log-ratios undefined; separate occurrence from ratios among positive detections and document censoring. [Aitchison (1982)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1982.tb01195.x) |
| How different are parcels on an instrument’s shared grid? | Total variation (TV) and Jensen–Shannon divergence (JS) | TV is the largest absolute probability difference over events formed from the cells. JS in bits measures how informative a cell is about parcel identity for equally likely parcels. Both handle empty cells. Neither assigns a smaller penalty to moving material into an adjacent empty cell than to a distant one. [Lin (1991)](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.61115) |
| How far did the distribution move? | Wasserstein or earth mover’s distance | Within size, transport between fractions 0.40 and 0.41 costs less than transport between 0.10 and 0.90. Joint transport across size and composition requires an explicit relative cost. For large clouds, one-dimensional projections can be compared efficiently; finite projection sets can miss differences. [Rubner, Tomasi and Guibas (2000)](https://ai.stanford.edu/~rubner/papers/rubnerIjcv00.pdf) · [Peyré and Cuturi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00567) |
| Can two multivariate samples be distinguished without binning? | Maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) | A characteristic kernel on size and composition distinguishes different population distributions in principle. Kernel length scales decide which differences finite samples resolve. Calibrate the sampling floor on repeated parcels or independent measurement blocks; shared particle ancestry makes an independent-particle test misleading. A significant difference gives little guidance on which diameter to measure. [Gretton et al. (2012)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v13/gretton12a.html) |
| Can someone reconstruct an observable from a small file? | Weighted representative particles or conditional quadrature | Store actual representative compositions and weights in each size interval, or fit nodes and weights to selected moments. Medoids retain physically realized particles; their assigned weights need an inventory check. Matched low moments are useful for smooth responses, while a sharp activation threshold can cut through a cluster. [Kaufman and Rousseeuw (1987)](https://wis.kuleuven.be/stat/robust/papers/publications-1987/kaufmanrousseeuw-clusteringbymedoids-l1norm-1987.pdf) · [Yuan and Fox (2011)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021999111004396) |

A four-species construction shows why the occurrence question earns a separate place. Suppose equal-size, equal-mass particles contain 50:50 pairs. In one population the pairs are A+B and C+D, equally frequent. In another they are A+C and B+D. Every species has the same bulk fraction and the same single-species histogram in both. Both have χ = 1/3, since each particle contains two effective species and the bulk contains four. But among particles containing A, the fraction carrying B is **100% versus 0%**. Replacing A and B with an absorbing core material and a possible coating constituent gives a concrete measurement question. Co-occurrence alone cannot establish coating geometry. [Optical sensitivity to aerosol mixing state](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/22/9265/2022/)

## Which comparisons earn their place in the six-hour test?

We evaluated the following quantities on the same 12 dry-size bins, with salt-rich and salt-lean as the two particle classes. Here a draw is a *particle by number*. The mass information budget above samples a chemical species by dry mass, so its values answer a different question.

| Number-weighted diagnostic | Hour 0 | Hour 3 | Hour 6 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Size–class mutual information in a single parcel (bits) | 0.5096 | 0.0376 | 0.0034 |
| JS between the parcels’ joint size–class histograms (bits) | 0.5096 | 0.0376 | 0.0034 |
| TV between those histograms | 0.6825 | 0.2056 | 0.0619 |
| Size-conditioned Wasserstein distance (salt-fraction units) | 0.5460 | 0.1644 | 0.0495 |

Four statistics give only two distinct numerical trends here. The two parcels exchange class labels and have the same size marginal. Their midpoint distribution therefore has independent size and class. In this special construction the divergence from that midpoint equals the mutual information in either parcel, which explains the identical JS and mutual-information rows. The salt fractions are fixed at 0.1 and 0.9, so the conditional transport cost is exactly 0.8 times TV. Neither equality holds in general. To reproduce all 13 checkpoints, run `PYTHONPATH=. python studies/parcel-composition/population_metrics.py` from the repository root. The script saves `population_metrics.csv` beside it and checks both identities and the mass-information budget.

## Testing the candidate descriptions

The [six-hour multispecies experiment](six-hour-aging.md) now compares weighted representative particles and compact χ profiles on activation and dry absorption. The broader comparison below extends that test to other descriptions and measurement limits.

Keep **χ, the between-size and within-size information in bits, and number-weighted size–composition profiles** as the reference report. Use a named species pairing when the question involves two materials on the same particle. For a quantity that another scientist must be able to recompute, compare the size–composition histogram with a small set of weighted representative particles at equal serialized bytes, including compositions, size edges and weights. Score absolute number and species-mass closure before scoring activation or optics.

Train bin edges or representative particles on some parcel histories and hold out entire histories with different source mixtures and aging. Score the **full activation curve** in percentage points, absorption at a stated wavelength under a fixed morphology model, and the largest error in a rare particle class. Repeat under realistic size resolution, counting statistics and species detection limits, with uncertainty across independent parcel histories. A useful outcome would be the smallest representation meeting prespecified errors in both activation and absorption. A split result would identify exactly which additional composition coordinate the second observable needs. Prior particle-resolved studies provide precedents for these two physical tests. [Ching et al., CCN activity](https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-17-7445-2017) · [Zaveri et al., optical and activation properties in an aging plume](https://doi.org/10.1029/2009JD013616) · [Paninski, finite-sample information estimation](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976603321780272)
