      Wind-driven emission from a finite dust bed

      How much dust a gust carries depends on how long the wind exceeds the erosion threshold and how much erodible material remains. In the two-gust dune experiment, a second gust of equal strength lifts fewer fine grains because the first exhausts that class. The source transfers dry particulate mass in kilograms between a finite surface patch and the airborne population. Two-gust example

      From wind at a reference height to vertical mass flux

      Section titled “From wind at a reference height to vertical mass flux”

      Sample the east and north components of the horizontal air velocity, and in m/s, at a declared physical height above the local bed. The dimensionless drag coefficient links this reference wind to a friction velocity and hence to the magnitude of surface shear stress for air density .

      The user supplies an onset threshold in m/s and a vertical emission coefficient in kg m⁻² s⁻¹ for each emitted class . This example uses the illustrative law

      is airborne dry mass emitted per unit erodible surface area and time. A threshold can depend on the size of surface grains, their cohesion and gravity. The Shao–Lu analysis treats loosely arranged spherical grains on a dry, bare surface. Saltating parent grains and the aerosols they liberate can have different diameters. Here the input classes specify the emitted aerosol, with an independently prescribed threshold and flux coefficient. Applying the law to field conditions requires size-resolved flux and surface observations. Shao and Lu (2000)

      Integrating a gust and depleting the bed

      Section titled “Integrating a gust and depleting the bed”

      For patch with erodible slope area in m² and available bed mass in kg, the accepted mass during an interval is

      Use the physical area covered by erodible material, including the represented crosswind thickness of a 2-D slice. Each patch samples its local wind at the horizontal center of its bottom cell. With componentwise linear wind between sounding records, is quadratic in time. The integral splits at sounding times and at crossings of the threshold. For example, a steadily reversing wind can pass below threshold and resume emitting during one output interval.

      The calculation integrates the flux before advancing the interval. Both airborne representations inject its integral at the left endpoint, an operator-splitting approximation for the positions and fates of newly emitted material. Refine accepted intervals to resolve the emission timing relative to deposition and boundary crossings. The offline source precomputes the schedule on the recorded grid. Resolved mode reserves one weighted slot for each nonzero patch–class–interval combination before any mass moves. The checkpoint records the accepted interval, stable record IDs and remaining patch masses.
