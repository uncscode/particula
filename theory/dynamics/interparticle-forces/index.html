      Dispersion and dielectric polarization in aerosol coagulation

      Brownian motion brings two spheres together. Whether they reach contact depends on the energy along their approach. Dispersion draws neutral particles together. A charged particle can also polarize a neutral neighbour. Two like-charged particles can experience a repulsive barrier outside an attractive well. The encounter rate depends on the whole path from large separation to contact. Evaluating the energy only at the surface misses the barrier.

      The figure uses 90 and 150 nm diameters, 298 K, 1 atm, density 2160 kg/m³, relative permittivity 5.9 and a Hamaker constant of 8.9 × 10⁻²⁰ J. The energy axis is cropped to show the barrier. The like-sign pair carries two elementary charges on each particle. Charge–neutral and opposite-sign cases carry one elementary charge on each indicated charged particle. The scalar material inputs are illustrative.

      Applying the model

      Section titled “Applying the model”

      Use one complete thermal encounter rate per pair. Turbulent shear and settling can supply additional mechanisms. Unit sticking, compact spheres, constant single-particle mobilities and nonretarded dispersion delimit the interpretation. Near-contact hydrodynamic resistance, large-separation retardation, aggregate shape and mixed-material dielectric evolution require additional physics.
