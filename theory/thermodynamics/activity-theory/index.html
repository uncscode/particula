      Particle-phase activity in kinetic condensation

      A droplet can evaporate in humid air or grow in subsaturated air. The direction depends on how the ambient vapor pressure compares with the vapor pressure at the droplet surface. Particle-phase water activity gives the composition contribution to that surface pressure. It changes as water or organic material enters or leaves the particle.

The Binary Activity Thermodynamics (BAT) model describes departures from ideality using a fit to the excess molar Gibbs energy. Composition varies along one coordinate for each fixed water–organic pair. The organic descriptors include molar mass, organic density, O:C and, where applicable, functional-group translation. The original fit used AIOMFAC calculations at 298.15 K. Particula’s binary activity function takes no temperature argument. Temperature still enters the pure-component vapor pressure and curvature correction. Gorkowski, Preston and Zuend (2019), sections 3.1–3.3

      Single liquids and miscibility gaps

      Section titled “Single liquids and miscibility gaps”

      A homogeneous activity curve can pass through compositions where two liquids are thermodynamically favored. The coexistence compositions have equal water activities and equal organic activities across phases. In a binary equilibrium calculation, a common tangent to the molar Gibbs energy gives the two endpoints, and the overall mole fraction determines their molar proportions by the lever rule. Between the endpoints, evaluating the homogeneous curve at the bulk composition gives a different physical state. Using coexistence activities in a kinetic droplet model also assumes internal redistribution is fast on the timescale of vapor exchange. Surface composition and internal transport require additional treatment when this assumption fails. BAT paper, Fig. 3 and section 4

      Evaluating the binary function

      Section titled “Evaluating the binary function”

      The functional API returns both activities, both mass fractions and both activity coefficients. The existing particle strategy ActivityNonIdealBinary.activity() returns organic activity.
