# Can particles bouncing off a dry tray explain the three-hour deposit?

At 2 m/s the trays gained 15.12 mg per tray between 30 minutes and three hours. The 10–20 µm fraction accounted for 6.13 mg of that gain. A model that let the tray become more adhesive could approach both gains and the measured volume median, in a scenario with an abrupt change in capture efficiency and, using the published tray dimensions, a larger local dust concentration than the centreline monitor recorded. The timing provides a separate test. By one hour the monitor had recorded 71% of the remaining airborne exposure, while the trays had acquired only 10% of their eventual additional mass. Measuring how many grains hit the tray during that interval would help distinguish delayed delivery from a change in what stuck. ## Still air and transfer to three winds

The reference surface captures a grain if its downward impact speed is below a size-dependent threshold. Above that speed the grain bounces. The threshold is 0.05 m/s at 10 µm and scales as the inverse square root of diameter, corresponding to adhesion energy proportional to area at fixed density. Impact speeds have a Gaussian normal component centred on the settling speed, with standard deviation equal to the profile-derived friction velocity. Only downward motion contributes to collisions, weighted by speed. In still air the fluctuation vanishes. The threshold, the ratio of speed spread to friction velocity, and the absence of local return are held fixed across fan settings. A friction velocity characterizes wall shear. Its use as the impact velocity spread is a scenario assumption. Microsphere impact experiments motivate a critical normal velocity. The dust–acrylic coefficients and the effects of grain shape, roughness and rolling are unresolved here. [Li, Dunn and Brach](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~pdunn/www.dunnpapers/99LDBjas.pdf) · [Model and implementation](rebound.py)

The following forecasts start with the measured early tray mass and volume spectrum. The closed model tracks the dust remaining in a 2 m column over the tray footprint at 30 minutes, with mixing and sidewall losses. The open model subjects the tray to the measured centreline concentration history and assumes the incoming airborne mass has the early tray's size distribution. This supplies a deliberately coarse incoming population for comparison. The table gives the three-hour volume median and the total mass gained after 30 minutes. All masses are mg per tray. Still air illustrates the importance of initial airborne composition. An open alternative inferred the airborne shape by dividing the early tray shape by settling speed. With the same rebound rule it gave 10.49 µm and a gain of 16.12 mg in still air, close to both means. At 2 m/s it gave 5.13 µm and 5.64 mg. At 2 m/s, 9.93 mg reached the reference open tray. Of this, 7.20 mg bounced, leaving 2.74 mg and a 6.57 µm median. Perfect capture of the same arrivals produced a 7.98 µm median and added 2.44 mg in the 10–20 µm band. Allowing 90% of bounced grains to return immediately for another independent impact yielded 6.13 mg total and 1.46 mg in the band. Dielectric image attraction with a prescribed 10 µC/m² contact-charge scale gave 6.40 and 1.61 mg, respectively. For comparison to the measured band, we interpolate the modeled cumulative mass at exactly 10 and 20 µm on log diameter and take the difference. Removal of grains already on a tray can change the median without increasing the retained mass. At 2 m/s a rate fitted to remove particles up to 10 µm at 0.215 per hour reproduced 9.80 µm while predicting a 4.45 mg net loss. At 6 m/s, Jia et al.’s supplementary screening found marked mobilization near the nominal test speed. Setting the removal rate to 0.1 per hour at 10 µm, increasing in direct proportion to diameter, brought the open-tray prediction down to 20.05 µm but produced an 8.96 mg loss. The endpoint replicates instead gained 13.65 mg. The workbook's kinetic sheet lists 22.725 mg below 63 µm at 30 minutes for this wind, while the endpoint replicates average 26.725 mg. We use the latter for the transfer table and keep the kinetic series on its own basis for timing. ## The monitor constrains when dust could have arrived

At 2 m/s the integral of centreline concentration over the final 150 minutes was only 6.99% of the initial 30-minute integral. If local airborne composition, the local-to-monitor concentration ratio and surface response all remained constant, the 25.84 mg present at 30 minutes would imply only 1.81 mg more by three hours. A cumulative-mass model forced to both endpoints and distributing the increment according to airborne exposure predicts 36.55 mg at one hour. The kinetic sheet gives 27.40 mg. The 9.15 mg difference has an approximate 95% interval of 3.21–15.09 mg. We propagated the three group standard errors with weights 1 − f, −1 and f for the early, one-hour and final retrievals, where f = 0.708. This calculation holds exposure fixed, assumes independent groups of five trays, and uses a Welch–Satterthwaite interval. The positions were assigned by retrieval time and shared a wind field. [Timing contrasts and intervals (`rebound-timing-intervals.csv`)](study-results.zip) At 1.5 hours the corresponding masses were 37.98 and 29.07 mg. The 10–20 µm band had reached 26% of its eventual gain by one hour, later than the exposure and earlier than the bulk mass. Filling the final four unmeasured minutes with zero concentration changes the fixed-response prediction by less than 0.02 mg. [Five sampling times (`rebound-timing.csv`)](study-results.zip) · [Monitor integral and tail checks (`rebound-bounds.csv`)](study-results.zip)

Per unit of integrated centreline exposure, the mean net accumulation increased 8.37-fold for all dust below 63 µm and 13.80-fold for 10–20 µm dust between the two windows. If only the sticking probability changed, even perfect late capture would require an early capture probability no greater than 12% overall and 7.2% in that band. These bounds assume stationary incident flux per unit monitor exposure and negligible detachment. [Threshold means (`threshold-masses.csv`)](study-results.zip) · [Monitor integrals (`rebound-bounds.csv`)](study-results.zip)

An optimistic delivery bound let every grain stick and placed all airborne mass in each disjoint size band at its fastest-settling upper edge. The minimum required fractions of airborne mass, summed across bands to meet the observed positive gains, were 8.51 times the available total for gravity alone. Allowing Gaussian downward fluctuations lowered that sum to one at a standard deviation of 0.090 m/s, or 1.43 times the friction velocity. Using the workbook’s larger tray area (0.05065 m² versus 0.03142 m² from the paper) lowered the gravity-only sum to 5.28 and the corresponding fluctuation speed to 0.041 m/s. These are conditional requirements from endpoint means and the centreline monitor. Local concentration, incident velocity and airborne composition above the trays were unmeasured. A larger local concentration would also lower the required fluctuation speed. [Delivery bounds (`rebound-delivery-limit.csv`)](study-results.zip)

Five distinct trays contributed to each endpoint at 2 m/s. The 95% Welch interval on the total gain is 7.06–23.19 mg. For the cumulative gain below 20 µm, the interval is 4.21–17.05 mg, spanning the 8.92 mg gravity ceiling for that threshold on the paper's area. Individual cumulative intervals share thresholds and cannot be combined as independent tests of the joint bound. Tray identifiers are missing across size thresholds. Feasible assignments give a standard error of 0.95–4.35 mg for the 10–20 µm gain. At 4 m/s only four late endpoint masses per threshold are present. The full channel spectra supply no replicate uncertainty. ## What if the tray became more adhesive?

Dust loading can change the forces holding particles on acrylic. Centrifugal experiments with polyethylene particles on acrylic coated with Arizona test dust provide an experimental precedent for loading-dependent retention. For Jia et al.’s grains the adhesion, normal impact speeds and charge histories remain unknown. We allowed a change in the capture threshold as a test of how much increased retention would be needed. In these scenarios the early tray spectrum determines a stationary airborne composition by size-specific inversion of the early capture rate. The absolute early mass determines a constant multiplier of the centreline concentration. Very weak early collection makes this inversion unstable. Some low-fluctuation cases require local multipliers above 100 million and have little physical interpretability. The three-hour data do not enter this calibration. [Lee et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/ina.12576) · [Surface scenarios (`rebound-changing-surface.csv`)](study-results.zip)

The imposed step occurs immediately after 30 minutes. Thresholds refer to 10 µm. For the image calculation a charged sphere approaches a flat dielectric of relative permittivity 3. The capture criterion includes both energy gained on approach and the work of escape, assuming a normal restitution coefficient of 0.5. The 10 µC/m² charge-transfer scale is an assumed input. Neither the experiment nor the bulk monitor establishes the onset of this force. The early calibration multipliers are area dependent. Holding the early capture velocities fixed, the larger workbook area rescales 1.28 and 1.94 to 0.79 and 1.20. The calibrated forecasts stay the same because the area and multiplier enter as a product. In the independent uncalibrated open run, using the larger area increases the 2 m/s gain from 2.74 to 4.41 mg and moves the median to 6.28 µm. [Changing surfaces (`rebound-changing-surface.csv`)](study-results.zip) · [Area sensitivity (`rebound-results.csv`)](study-results.zip)

Timing matters even for a surface with the same eventual adhesion. At twice the friction velocity, gradually increasing the mechanical threshold with a 15-minute time constant gave a median of 8.11 µm, a 10.07 mg gain and 3.07 mg gained at 10–20 µm. Time constants of 60 and 150 minutes reduced the total gains to 6.09 and 3.95 mg. Ramping up charge over 15 minutes at the same velocity spread gave 7.98 µm, 5.02 mg total and 1.50 mg in the band. These responses follow from the measured decline in airborne concentration. A rapid increase in capture while grains are still available has more effect. Paired clean and dust-coated collectors with recorded near-tray incident and rebounding flux by size would test the surface explanation independently of the centreline proxy. ## Reproduce the tests

From this study directory, follow the environment and input instructions, run the base study to produce its numerical outputs, then run the command below. The study includes 317 fixed-surface configurations, 36 two-stage comparisons and 12 gradual transitions. The surface calculations used dry, fixed-size grains and disabled grain–grain coagulation to isolate impact and retention. The earlier collision and humidity cases are in the [transport study](transport.md). The closed model uses matrix exponentials on 12 vertical layers, with a 24-layer sensitivity. Open-model exposure uses three-point Gaussian quadrature over the minute-resolved concentration trace and holds its last value through the missing tail. Closed inventories include suspended dust, material on the tray, wall deposits and losses out of the footprint. The open balance accounts separately for arrivals, bounces and subsequent removal. [Replay instructions](README.md) · [Calculation](rebound.py) · [Test specification](rebound-spec.md)

```bash
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 "$JIA_PY" rebound.py
```
