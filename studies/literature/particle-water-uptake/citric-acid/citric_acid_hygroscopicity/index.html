      How accurately does BAT predict citric-acid droplet water activity?

      Section titled “How accurately does BAT predict citric-acid droplet water activity?”

      Marsh et al. inferred water activity and solute mass fraction for aqueous citric acid droplets at 293.15 K. We compare all 12 points in their Table S9.2 with the parameters used for the citric-acid BAT curve in Gorkowski et al., Figure 2b. The parameters are fixed before evaluating the measurements. The final calculation asks whether finite-rate exchange approaches the same water content from either direction.

      Python · section 1

      Evaluate particle activity at measured composition

      Section titled “Evaluate particle activity at measured composition”

      Use the authors’ mass ratio and O:C (1.16667). The common mass scale cancels when BAT forms a mole fraction. Evaluate both edges of the composition error bar and compare that prediction interval with the asymmetric experimental water-activity interval. The observed mass-fraction range lies on the monotonic homogeneous branch for this compound.

      Python · section 2

      Relax a droplet in a fixed water-vapor reservoir

      Section titled “Relax a droplet in a fixed water-vapor reservoir”

      Use isothermal continuum diffusion, with water mass rate . The saturation vapor density is 0.0173 kg/m³ at 293.15 K and D is 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s. Dry radius is 5 µm. These choices set the time scale of this diagnostic. The flat-interface pressure, prescribed reservoir and nonvolatile solute isolate the long-time composition. The measurement supplies no relaxation time for this test. Integrating the log water-to-solute mass ratio preserves positive masses. Each reservoir receives the opposite of the droplet’s water-mass change.

      Check the native condensation and restart workflow

      Section titled “Check the native condensation and restart workflow”

      At the measured activity nearest 0.75, integrate the two initial droplets with Particula’s isothermal transport and ROS3 solver. The water reservoir is held fixed, and the Kelvin correction uses a constant 0.072 N/m surface tension and 998.2 kg/m³ surface density. Compare against an independent root with that same curvature correction. Record accepted states and continue from a checkpoint at 10 s to test reproduction of the 2000 s endpoint. The experimental comparison above uses bulk activity.
