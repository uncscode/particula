      Fit a growth trajectory from light alone

      Section titled “Fit a growth trajectory from light alone”

      Can bulk extinction and absorption measured during condensation recover two starting radii when the populations draw from the same vapor reservoir? This synthetic example fits both radii using CUDA. The measurements supply neither radius nor vapor concentration during growth. An independent radius equation generates the observations and provides those withheld histories for checking the fit.

      Prescribe the closed box

      Section titled “Prescribe the closed box”

      Two monodisperse populations have known number densities of 300 and 700 cm⁻³. A vapor starts at 10 mg m⁻³ and condenses with unit accommodation onto spherical particles with a material density of 1000 kg m⁻³. The isothermal model holds diffusivity constant, sets equilibrium vapor at the surface to zero, and conserves gas plus particle mass. Each sphere has a prescribed absolute refractive index of 1.52 + 0.03i across the three vacuum wavelengths. The fit integrates 224 equal steps over 1.4 s.

      The calculation requires a CUDA device for both optimizers. Condensation rate and transition correction · Sphere optics conventions

      Python · section 1

      Generate optical data from a radius equation

      Section titled “Generate optical data from a radius equation”

      The reference solver evolves radius directly. It evaluates remaining gas from the volume added to both particle populations, so a larger uptake by one population leaves less vapor for the other. Radius is in µm internally and converted to metres before calling the CPU Mie solver. Each optical datum is a number-density-weighted sum of optical cross sections. The synthetic observations have no added noise. The objective scales each extinction and absorption residual by 1% of its observation. Scattering is available in the model output and is excluded from the loss.

      Python · section 2

      Fit the same loss with two derivative methods

      Section titled “Fit the same loss with two derivative methods”

      Both bounded optimizers use the same three starting points. The autodiff fit seeds the reverse pass with the derivative of the scalar loss with respect to each optical output. The seed uses the array layout (time, box, wavelength, channel). Its scattering entries are zero. The reverse pass replays the trajectory in 16-step segments from mass checkpoints. The finite-difference fit uses centered, three-point differences in the interior and a one-sided stencil near a bound. Each count below covers all starts and includes the forward pass inside each adjoint call.

      Python · section 3

      Check the gradient and the withheld physical histories

      Section titled “Check the gradient and the withheld physical histories”

      Test a centered directional difference at a starting point, before fitting. Replay each optimized radius pair with a separate radius observation to check the radius and vapor histories against the continuous solution at the true initial radii. Reconstruct vapor from the replayed radii by mass balance to check sensitivity to depletion. Also replay on a 64-times finer mesh with the inferred initial radii held fixed. This separates timestep error in vapor depletion from the error in the recovered radii. The checks allow 2 nm of initial-radius error, 1 nm of trajectory-radius error on the fit mesh, and 0.1 µg m⁻³ of vapor error on the refined mesh. Timings cover warm, synchronized fits. The mass and gas payload estimate is 1,280 B for checkpointed autodiff and 56 B for finite differences. It counts trajectory arrays and their adjoints. The measured CUDA pool high-water mark covers the combined workload, including optics scratch allocated through the pool.

      Both derivative methods can find the initial radii when the optical response, number densities, refractive index, and growth law are prescribed. Across six fits the largest initial-radius error is 0.0412 nm. The withheld radius error reaches 0.0419 nm and the vapor error reaches 1.26 µg m⁻³ on the fitted mesh. Refinement reduces the latter to 0.043 µg m⁻³. An adjoint call adds checkpoint replay to its forward evaluation, so a lower count of forward calls need not imply a shorter fit. Broader size distributions or unknown composition could change the inference. A follow-up could withhold one wavelength and compare its prediction while fitting refractive index as a nuisance parameter.
