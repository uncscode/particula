      Record aerosol extinction at 10 Hz during hygroscopic growth

      Section titled “Record aerosol extinction at 10 Hz during hygroscopic growth”

      Can a simulation save the optical transient without saving each aerosol state? We follow water uptake in a closed, isothermal box and record bulk extinction, scattering, and absorption every 0.1 s at 450, 550, and 700 nm. Every row also carries relative humidity from the live gas water. Sparse aerosol snapshots from separate runs provide an offline calculation at matching times. A second comparison measures the cost of writing the same number of optical and native-array observations.

      The case uses 192 weighted particle-resolved slots with dry radii between 80 and 260 nm and a total number density of 2 × 10¹⁰ m⁻³. Each slot starts with water volume equal to 35% of its solute volume. The gas starts at 83% relative humidity and can lose water to particles. Temperature and pressure stay at 298.15 K and 101325 Pa. A small accommodation coefficient (3 × 10⁻⁶) spreads the hygroscopic transient over several seconds so that the observation cadence is visible. The parameters illustrate the recording workflow and do not describe a specific aerosol or instrument.

      The setup uses native Particula arrays and the isothermal condensation runnable. The optical configuration combines illustrative species indices by volume with the Lorentz–Lorenz rule. The result assumes independent scattering by homogeneous spheres. This example runs NumPy and native CUDA explicitly and requires a CUDA device. See the condensation theory for transport and the optics guide for the optical mixing and reduction conventions.

      Python · section 1

      Give optics and native state different output schedules

      Section titled “Give optics and native state different output schedules”

      The CUDA owner advances one 0.1 s cycle using four internal condensation substeps. On NumPy we set a 0.025 s maximum step and land on the same observation grid. The NumPy time driver can shorten a step to reach a requested time. For each backend we use the same physics schedule with and without recording.

      The optical observer evaluates the current population and writes reduced coefficients in chunks of 32 rows. Dense optical recording needs no detached native state, and the CUDA path transfers only bulk coefficients and status for the observer. The native-array comparison records particle masses, represented counts, charge, gas water, and fixed environmental coordinates. Its uncompressed analysis archive retains arrays for reanalysis. It uses the same 10 Hz output grid.
