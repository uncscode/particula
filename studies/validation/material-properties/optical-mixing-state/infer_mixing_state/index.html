      Can bulk optics resolve aerosol mixing state?

      Section titled “Can bulk optics resolve aerosol mixing state?”

      A chemical sample can tell us how much of each constituent was present. The same dry mass and size distribution can describe particles that each contain both constituents or a population of separate pure particles. Can light distinguish those cases? This synthetic study compares a whole-population optical fit with a fit that also uses two ideal size cuts. It uses homogeneous spheres, Lorentz–Lorenz volume mixing, subsaturated κ-Köhler growth, and the Particula optics guide’s conventions. The two materials have prescribed, illustrative values for complex index, density, and κ. The inverse calculations use a CUDA device and the Warp optical adjoint.

      Prescribe dry inventory and optical measurements

      Section titled “Prescribe dry inventory and optical measurements”

      Start with a bimodal dry distribution and convert dry mass concentrations and material densities into volume fractions. Scale the size distribution to the resulting total dry volume. This makes the sum of the mass in the size bins agree with the bulk chemistry, in kg/m³. Measurement groups specify vacuum wavelength, RH as a fraction, and a dry diameter cut. Use an ideal transmission of one inside each selected band and zero outside. These channels represent integrated optical coefficients within the cut, in m⁻¹. The last three channels are held out of every fit.

      Generate observations from independent population sums

      Section titled “Generate observations from independent population sums”

      If a dry bin contains particles per cubic metre, an internal fraction assigns particles to the homogeneous mixture and particles to each pure material , with dry volume fraction . Every subpopulation starts at the same dry diameter. Therefore the dry bin number and each dry species inventory are independent of . Verify this at the pure internal and pure external endpoints and at the specified truth. Then let each subpopulation equilibrate separately before summing its Mie cross sections. This reference uses NumPy with explicit number and mass accounting. Both forward models share the same physical assumptions.

      For the strong-contrast case, assign the first material a higher index, stronger absorption, and weaker hygroscopicity than the second. For the weak case, assign similar indices and hygroscopicities to both. Use noiseless central values with an assumed 1% independent standard deviation per channel. The fit then measures sensitivity to that error scale without depending on one random noise draw.

      Fit the same measurements with one or two fractions

      Section titled “Fit the same measurements with one or two fractions”

      Fit one common fraction first. Then fit two fractions, using the prescribed 235 nm boundary. Compare the effects of adding dry size selection or changing the allocation of dry material. For each two-band fit, scan one fraction from zero to one and refit the other. The width of the range within the displayed profile cutoff reflects the stated uncertainty scale and model. Near physical bounds this nominal cutoff is a sensitivity diagnostic. The unused 633 nm channels test predictions at another RH and wavelength.

      Read the profiles and predictions

      Section titled “Read the profiles and predictions”

      The whole-population fit recovers the prescribed fractions from the noiseless signals. At the assumed 1% precision, its small-band profile still spans 0–1. With size cuts, the nominal profile ranges are about 0.015–0.35 for the small band and 0.76–0.88 for the large band. The weak-contrast case admits the full range in both bands even with size selection. The dash-dot line shows the nominal cutoff, and the vertical dotted lines locate the generating fractions.

      A single common fraction underpredicts withheld small-band absorption by 5.5 assumed standard deviations. Reassigning the same bulk species mass by size shifts the fit to the upper small-band bound and overpredicts the same held-out coefficient by 16.3 standard deviations. The poorer predictions also show up in the training residuals, with χ² about 62 for one fraction and 226 for the reassigned composition over six fitted channels. All comparisons use synthetic observations from the two-band, uniform-composition model.
