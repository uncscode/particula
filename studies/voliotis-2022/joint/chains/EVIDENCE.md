# Which later-generation reactions have experimental analogues?

A dimer can disappear during oxidation while much of its material remains suspended. That distinction matters here because Manchester’s aerosol mass and number measurements cannot identify the reactant, the broken bond or the fraction of products that evaporated. The entries below specify the experimentally observed part of each pathway. The fitted pools represent effective organic material shared across experiments. Their fitted first-order rates incorporate oxidant exposure, composition and, where relevant, phase transfer.

| Candidate reaction family | What the primary experiment established | Scope of the comparison |
|---|---|---|
| α-Pinene intermediates form a dimer | In α-pinene ozonolysis, isotope substitution and gas-phase mass spectrometry supported accretion of oxygenated C₁₀ peroxy radicals to a C₂₀ product. Separate measurements with authentic standards identified specific particle-phase dimer esters and established condensed-phase accretion for those molecules. [Gas-phase isotope study](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.2c10398) · [Particle ester structures](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi0857) | Distinct dimer structures can arise in either phase. Bulk mass contains no direct information about which population supplied an aged product. |
| An α-pinene dimer reacts with oxidant | Heterogeneous OH experiments tracked dimer ester signals including C₁₇H₂₆O₈ and C₁₉H₂₈O₇. In aqueous extracts, OH depleted the 358 and 368 Da dimers while several ring-opened acids increased. [Heterogeneous OH exposures](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jes.2023.08.021) · [Aqueous OH experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10116591/) | Dimer removal has substrate-specific support. Each daughter’s parent, phase fraction and rate in the Manchester chamber remain unresolved. Oxygenation can also leave an aged product suspended. |
| An α-pinene dimer cleaves, hydrolyzes or rearranges | Acidic and basic aqueous extracts showed slow dimer hydrolysis and increased terpenylic acid. In another experiment, water exposure gave small overall compositional changes. Zhang et al. observed a C₁₇ ester dimer and pinic acid, with the latter continuing to form after parent consumption. They proposed rapid condensed-phase decomposition of an organic diacyl peroxide as one source. The peroxide step was inferred from product histories and a fit. An effective cleavage rate or a volatile end product remains hypothetical in Manchester. |
| α-Pinene material released to the gas reacts again | A chamber study of authentic pinonic acid and of α-pinene SOA detected MBTCA after OH exposure. MBTCA yield tracked the gas-phase pinonic acid fraction when temperature changed. [AIDA gas-phase aging](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/12/1483/2012/) | This supports a gas-to-low-volatility return pathway for a named terpene oxidation product. | An isoprene oligomer produces smaller suspended species or volatile fragments | Four hours of heterogeneous OH exposure of IEPOX-derived particles yielded composition consistent with oligomer breakdown and replenishment of low-volatility monomers and fragments. Aqueous photooxidation of 2-methyltriolsulfate, a specific isoprene-derived monomer, yielded formic and acetic acids. [Oligomer aging](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.2c03200) · [Oxygenated volatile products](https://doi.org/10.1039/D3EA00076A) | The first study used acid-driven IEPOX particle formation. ## Evidence grades for all 40 trials

The table of individual fits pairs every trial number and channel key with a primary-source observation, an evidence grade, and the unverified parts of the equation. **Fifteen trials have a reaction-family analogue and 25 have unverified complete routes.** A family analogue requires published aging of a corresponding substrate class that supports the proposed direction of reaction. The unverified designation applies when evidence covers only precursor production, a distant substrate, separate disconnected reactions, or a different phase. All 40 lack a precise molecular equivalent for the complete model equation, its product branching and its assignment to the Manchester chamber. In particular, molecular evidence is missing for the aging of the proposed mixed α-pinene/o-cresol dimer (AC) and the material formed by α-pinene vapor uptake (AU). The nominally neutral Manchester seed differs from the acidic sulfate on which pinonaldehyde produced higher-mass species. [Trial-by-trial evidence](evidence.csv) · [Pinonaldehyde uptake experiments](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005JD006978)

Concurrent loss of dimer signals and formation of monomers in a mixture containing cresol provide a closer analogue for cleavage of aromatic particle dimers. Those changes occurred in nitrate-radical experiments and were much weaker in the OH experiments. For α-pinene, independent isothermal evaporation experiments gave a fitted 9–28 h decomposition lifetime for reversibly formed oligomers or other reactive constituents in dark, vapor-free and oxidant-free conditions. Particle formula time series in a separate α-pinene ozonolysis experiment showed some dimers peaking within the first hour and others later. These cases motivate chemistry and competing physical interpretations without fixing a rate or vapor yield for the Manchester reservoirs. [Aromatic-mixture aging](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.3c00225) · [Dark α-pinene evaporation](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsearthspacechem.8b00084) · [α-Pinene dimer time series](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aax8922)

## A check against the same campaign

The Manchester companion study reported an average OH estimate of (8 ± 3) × 10⁵ molecules cm⁻³, 50 ± 5% relative humidity and nominally neutral sulfate seed. Its thermodenuder inversion gave 29% low-volatility and 71% semivolatile particle mass in the ternary system. The corresponding particle ion signal gave 6% low-volatility material. These values depend on instrument response and on the assumed volatility retrieval. The FIGAERO-CIMS measurements reported in that paper came from the last cycle, more than five hours after lights-on. A direct comparison would require extending the model to the sampling interval and specifying how each product class responds to the instrument. [Manchester measurement methods and ternary fractions](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/22/13677/2022/)

## A measurement that could distinguish the chains

An observable specific to dimer aging would pair the loss of a molecularly identified dimer with the appearance of its labeled products in each phase. For the α-pinene channel, follow particle dimer esters, volatile fragments and retained oxygenated acids through and beyond the mass peak. Light–dark or oxidant perturbations with measured humidity and particle area would separate changes in effective reaction rates from changing physical partitioning. In mixed experiments, labeling one precursor would establish whether carbon from both appears in the proposed dimer and later fragments. Manchester’s published molecular-composition and volatility observations already provide independent constraints on the predicted product classes.
