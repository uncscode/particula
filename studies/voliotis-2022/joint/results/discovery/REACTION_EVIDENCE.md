# Twenty fitted chamber reactions and their closest published analogues

A, C and I indicate α-pinene, o-cresol and isoprene. G denotes the gas intermediate from the original delay model, V the secondary oxygenated-vapor reservoir, P the labile suspended organic product, S a stabilized suspended product and F terminal volatile products. The rate multiplies the effective pool concentration in the model. A match to a reaction family supports chemical plausibility. Each entry records which molecular identities, rates or conditions the cited paper establishes.

### 1. G_A + G_C → S_AC

Gas-phase cross-reactions of oxygenated peroxy radicals from α-pinene ozonolysis, yielding low-volatility organic dimers. Speciated RO₂ and gas-phase C16–20 dimer measurements, together with concentration and radical-chemistry perturbations, implicated RO₂ cross-reactions in dimer production. [Quantitative constraints on autoxidation and dimer formation from direct probing of monoterpene-derived peroxy radical chemistry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6275546/)

**Scope of the match.** An o-cresol-derived reaction partner, the identity of the mixed product, immediate complete transfer to stable aerosol and a rate coefficient for lumped G_A and G_C were not established.

The final effective rate was 2.5557 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0.059499 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 2. 2 P_A → S_AA,p

Particle-phase addition of a pinene-derived alcohol to a cyclic acylperoxyhemiacetal to form dimer esters in α-pinene SOA. Authentic standards established structures of major ester dimers, and experiments located their formation in particles from pinene ozonolysis. [Particle-phase accretion forms dimer esters in pinene secondary organic aerosol](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi0857)

**Scope of the match.** The two reactants have distinct structures despite being represented by one P_A pool. The bulk rate, irreversible retention and fraction of Manchester organic mass converted remain unmeasured.

The final effective rate was 0 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 1.507e-17 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 3. P_A → F_A,p

Irradiation of particle-bound peroxides and other chromophores in α-pinene ozonolysis SOA, followed by loss of volatile products. Near-UV irradiation of denuded particles depleted organic peroxides; the inferred particle-diameter loss was 0.56% per day under the authors' solar scaling. [Direct Photolysis of α-Pinene Ozonolysis Secondary Organic Aerosol: Effect on Particle Mass and Peroxide Content](https://doi.org/10.1021/es502350u)

**Scope of the match.** The experiment did not measure one fragmentation coefficient for every particle constituent. Its lamp, peroxide abundance and exposure differ from the illuminated Manchester mixture.

The final effective rate was 1.0055 h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0.13695 h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 4. P_I → F_I,p

Near-UV irradiation of isoprene-derived SOA producing gas-phase small oxygenated organics, including formic acid. An irradiated film of chamber-generated isoprene ozonolysis SOA emitted low molecular weight oxygenated products measured in the gas stream by PTR-ToF-MS. [Photodegradation of Secondary Organic Aerosol Particles as a Source of Small, Oxygenated Volatile Organic Compounds](https://aerosol.chem.uci.edu/publications/Irvine/2016_Malecha_EST_SOA_photodegradation.pdf)

**Scope of the match.** Collected material, film thickness, illumination and precursor oxidation conditions differed from suspended Manchester particles. The gas signal assignments and bulk rate are uncertain.

The final effective rate was 3.4237 h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 3.1644 h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 5. V_A → S_A,upt

Gas-phase pinonaldehyde reacting after uptake on sulfuric acid/ammonium sulfate particles, giving oligomeric particle material. Particle spectra showed fragments heavier than the pinonaldehyde monomer after vapor exposure. Uptake depended strongly on acidity and was absent on neutral ammonium sulfate in the authors' controls. [Reactive uptake of pinonaldehyde on acidic aerosols](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005JD006978)

**Scope of the match.** V_A is an unresolved pool rather than identified pinonaldehyde. Nominally neutral Manchester seed differs from the acidic seed that gave reactive uptake; neither an uptake coefficient nor permanent coating retention transfers directly.

The final effective rate was 0.73072 h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0.23852 h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 6. G_C → V_C

Gas-phase OH oxidation and ring opening of toluene and 1,2,4-trimethylbenzene to glyoxal, methylglyoxal and other oxygenated fragments. Product measurements in aromatic photooxidation identified first-generation ring-opening dicarbonyls from these aromatic precursors. [Mechanistic study of the formation of ring-retaining and ring-opening products from the oxidation of aromatic compounds under urban atmospheric conditions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7133713/)

**Scope of the match.** The measured parent aromatics differ from o-cresol, whose oxidation also has strong ring-retaining channels. No o-cresol-specific cleavage yield, delayed reservoir lifetime or conversion of all reactant mass to one oxygenated vapor follows.

The final effective rate was 3.2365e-19 h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0.05111 h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 7. 2 G_C → S_CC

Gas-phase radical accretion following toluene OH oxidation, including oxygenated C7 peroxy radicals and C14 dimeric products. Gas-phase CIMS detected rapidly forming dimer signals tracking aromatic peroxy radicals. Dimer formulas were consistent with both RO₂ self- and cross-reactions. [Oxygenated organic molecules produced by low-NOx photooxidation of aromatic compounds: contributions to secondary organic aerosol and steric hindrance](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/24/2099/2024/)

**Scope of the match.** The experiments used aromatic hydrocarbon parents under low NOx. They did not establish an o-cresol radical-pair identity, complete retention after condensation or the fitted lumped rate in Manchester.

The final effective rate was 0 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0.011264 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 8. 2 G_I → S_II

Two isoprene-derived hydroxyperoxy radicals HO–C₅H₈O₂ form gaseous C₁₀H₁₈O₄ accretion product plus O₂. Direct sampling of products from OH + isoprene identified the C₁₀ accretion product assigned to the first-generation hydroxyperoxy radical self-reaction. [First oxidation products from the reaction of hydroxyl radicals with isoprene for pristine environmental conditions](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42004-019-0120-9)

**Scope of the match.** The Manchester lump aggregates many oxidation products and immediately assigns the accretion flux to stable suspended material; the paper measured gaseous products and does not establish that condensed yield or rate.

The final effective rate was 6.473e-18 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0.00028351 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 9. V_I → S_I,upt

Gas-phase isoprene epoxydiol reactive uptake onto sulfate-containing aerosol. Gaston et al. measured over 500-fold greater uptake on ammonium bisulfate than on ammonium sulfate, where the coefficient was at most 10⁻⁴. [Reactive Uptake of an Isoprene-Derived Epoxydiol to Submicron Aerosol Particles](https://doi.org/10.1021/es5034266)

**Scope of the match.** V_I is not measured IEPOX, and Manchester supplied nominally neutral sulfate. Acidity, organic coating, available surface and gas-phase precursor abundance control whether uptake contributes.

The final effective rate was 9.8713e-18 h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 1.0706e-17 h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 10. V_A → S_A,ox

Gas-phase pinonaldehyde + OH generates organic products that form secondary aerosol. Low-NOx photooxidation of pinonaldehyde in chamber experiments produced measurable SOA; varying the seed and aerosol loading affected the inferred yield. [Photo-oxidation of pinonaldehyde at low NOx: from chemistry to organic aerosol formation](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/13/3227/2013/)

**Scope of the match.** The fitted vapor pool is not a pinonaldehyde measurement, and the Manchester oxidants, yield, gas-to-particle transfer delay and retained mass need separate constraints.

The final effective rate was 0 h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0 h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 11. P_C → F_C,p

Near-UV photodegradation of collected SOA from 1,3,5-trimethylbenzene oxidation, releasing small oxygenated gas products. The study detected small volatile oxygenates over irradiated films of chamber SOA, including substituted aromatic precursor systems, and found substantial precursor dependence. [Photodegradation of Secondary Organic Aerosol Particles as a Source of Small, Oxygenated Volatile Organic Compounds](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.6b02313)

**Scope of the match.** The analogue is methylated-aromatic SOA rather than pure o-cresol SOA. Identical photoproducts, irradiation rate, aerosol morphology and mass loss cannot be assumed.

The final effective rate was 2.1846e-18 h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0.098767 h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 12. V_C → S_C,ox

Gas-phase OH addition to o-cresol-derived 3-methylcatechol and successive phenolics produces more oxygenated toluenes and methylbenzoquinones, some of which enter particles. CIMS and particle DART-MS detected later-generation oxygenated products from cresol oxidation. Separate 3-methylcatechol oxidation experiments traced the next generation of products. [Formation of highly oxygenated low-volatility products from cresol oxidation](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/17/3453/2017/)

**Scope of the match.** V_C pools compounds spanning many volatilities and the single irreversible S_C flux omits explicitly oxidant-dependent rates, multiple generations and repartitioning.

The final effective rate was 0 h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 9.1837e-18 h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 13. 2 G_A → S_AA

Gas-phase accretion of two C₁₀H₁₅O₁₀ peroxy radicals from α-pinene ozonolysis to form C₂₀H₃₀O₁₈. Gas-phase mass spectrometry with isotope substitutions supported accretion from α-pinene peroxy radicals, including a 20-carbon product retaining the expected exchangeable hydrogens. [Large Gas-Phase Source of Esters and Other Accretion Products in the Atmosphere](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.2c10398)

**Scope of the match.** The chemical identity and concentration of G_A are unresolved in the fit. A gas-phase accretion product need not immediately or entirely reach suspended particles.

The final effective rate was 0.21412 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 14. 2 P_I → S_II,p

Particle-phase ester formation involving isoprene-derived 2-methylglyceric acid units following methacrolein oxidation at high NOx. Chamber SOA product analysis linked 2-methylglyceric acid monomer units to ester-linked higher oligomers in the high-NOx pathway. [Chemical Composition of Secondary Organic Aerosol Formed from the Photooxidation of Isoprene](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/a5pcq-9gb04)

**Scope of the match.** This supports accretion of particular particle constituents, not conversion of all particle organics at one quadratic rate. Humidity, precursor branching, and the negligible suspended mass in Manchester's isoprene-only controls restrict transfer.

The final effective rate was 0 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 15. V_C → S_C,upt

Glyoxal vapor reacts during uptake onto deliquesced aqueous ammonium sulfate particles, including formation of nitrogen-containing condensed products. Simultaneous gas- and particle-phase chemical speciation tracked glyoxal uptake and chemical transformation on sulfate seed at relative humidity at or above about 80%. [Online Speciation of Glyoxal Multiphase Reactions on Deliquesced Ammonium Sulfate Particles](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsearthspacechem.4c00369)

**Scope of the match.** The experiment supplied glyoxal directly rather than identifying the o-cresol-derived V_C mixture. Liquid water, reversibility, and the yield of persistent material near 50% RH are unresolved.

The final effective rate was 0 h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0 h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 16. G_A → V_A

Gas-phase α-pinene OH oxidation forms pinonaldehyde through its peroxy-radical chemistry and subsequent oxidation or photolysis of hydroxy hydroperoxides. Gas-phase analyses detected pinonaldehyde in controlled α-pinene oxidation; further conversion of hydroperoxide intermediates contributed to the low-NOx total pinonaldehyde yield. [α-pinene photooxidation under controlled chemical conditions – Part 1: Gas-phase composition in low- and high-NOx environments](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/12/6489/2012/)

**Scope of the match.** The fitted G_A delay, V_A molecular mixture and yield are bulk parameters; effective formation rates in the mixed-O₃/OH Manchester runs were not measured.

The final effective rate was 0.14319 h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0 h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 17. V_A → F_A,g

Sunlight photolysis of gaseous pinonaldehyde to lower-carbon gas products, tested separately from OH oxidation. In the OH-scavenged sunlight run the reported formaldehyde yield was 0.18 ± 0.20 and no acetone formation was observed. Pinonaldehyde itself decayed, and the measured gas products tested the proposed fragmentation mechanism. [Photooxidation of pinonaldehyde at ambient conditions investigated in the atmospheric simulation chamber SAPHIR](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/20/13701/2020/)

**Scope of the match.** V_A is an ensemble, and a single effective loss coefficient neglects parent-specific photolysis frequencies, spectrum changes and branching into other products.

The final effective rate was 1.6035 h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0 h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 18. G_A + G_I → F_AI

Cross-termination of α-pinene- and isoprene-derived gas-phase peroxy radicals giving C14–15 products at the expense of C19–20 monoterpene dimers. CLOUD experiments measured a shift from C19–20 toward C14–15 dimers and suppressed early particle formation upon adding isoprene, consistent with mixed RO₂ termination. [Molecular understanding of the suppression of new-particle formation by isoprene](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/20/11809/2020/)

**Scope of the match.** The C14–15 products can still participate in growth. Treating all material as permanently volatile F_AI is a model end member; Manchester NOx, loading, and suspended seed differ.

The final effective rate was 2.0025 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 19. G_I → V_I

Gas-phase isoprene hydroxyperoxy radicals reacting under HO₂-dominated conditions produce methyl vinyl ketone and methacrolein. Continuous-flow chamber photooxidation and selective reagent-ion measurements distinguished the two C₄ carbonyl products and determined their yields. [Production of methyl vinyl ketone and methacrolein via the hydroperoxyl pathway of isoprene oxidation](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/13/5715/2013/)

**Scope of the match.** The lump G_I combines multiple isomers and fates. Manchester's NO/HO₂ competition, additional fragments and gas product yields are not constrained by the suspended mass data.

The final effective rate was 5.2593e-19 h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0 h⁻¹ in the joint fit.

### 20. 2 P_C → S_CC,p

Aqueous guaiacol- and syringol-derived phenoxyl radical coupling through C–C or C–O bonds yields dimers and higher oligomers. Mass spectra of organics generated by aqueous phenolic oxidation showed dimer and higher oligomer products consistent with phenoxyl coupling. [Insights into secondary organic aerosol formed via aqueous-phase reactions of phenolic compounds based on high resolution mass spectrometry](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/10/4809/2010/)

**Scope of the match.** The analogue used identifiable methoxyphenols in water. The abundance and reactivity of condensed o-cresol-derived phenolics, particle water content, two-body rate and product persistence in the Manchester experiments remain unverified.

The final effective rate was 7.0384e-20 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the mass fit and 0 m³ air µmol⁻¹ h⁻¹ in the joint fit.
