# Particle-resolved Manchester chamber calibration

## State and observations

Each occupied slot is one physical particle in a finite, well-mixed air sample of volume V = N₀ / n₀. Here N₀ is the number of simulated seed particles and n₀ the experiment’s initial particle number per cubic metre. The 15 runs have separate gas reservoirs and sampling volumes. A survivor carries its sulfate and 19 nonoverlapping organic mass inventories. These include three labile precursor pools, an unlabelled stable pool, and five product families each with an original particle product, an oxidized product, and a retained fragment. The five corresponding volatile daughters join the common gas. Active particles can have the same diameter and different compositions. Condensed and gas products are the previously defined lumped chemical species. We track dry mass equivalents using 1.77 g cm⁻³ for sulfate and 1.40 g cm⁻³ for organics.

For each run, seed masses start at quantiles of a lognormal *number* distribution. A common geometric standard deviation (GSD) is fitted. We rescale the individual masses to the measured seed mass and use the initial count from the preceding study. This normalization treats the initial instrument count as total seed number. If reconstructed seeds lie outside the detector window, especially below the 40 nm DMPS cutoff, simulated visible number starts below the measured count by construction. Seed GSD and births can compensate for that discrepancy. Hydrated diameter, spatial mixing gradients and shape-dependent mobility are unresolved. The particle population evolves at full simulation resolution; histogram bins are used only to compare it with instruments. [Original chamber records](../data/CAMPAIGN_SERIES.md) · [Particle-resolved modeling precedent](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008JD011073)

## Chemistry and kernels

Each minute’s source comes from the archived precursor disappearance histories, inherited dose ranges and mixed-precursor source terms. The shared gas block supplies material to individual particles. A fitted number between zero and one interpolates between allocations proportional to surface area and dry volume. The area-scaled uptake reactions respond to the instantaneous total surface area. Each particle advances the admitted condensed reactions on its own inventories. For particle dimerization, a particle’s concentration equivalent is its organic pool contribution divided by its share of total dry volume; equal composition therefore recovers the old bulk second-order rate law. This extension of a bulk-fitted rate to individual particles is an assumption. Volatile daughters return to the shared gas at the next split step. The unaltered 70-channel bank defines all equations, product labels and allowed rates. [Reaction mechanism](../chains/METHODS.md) · [Particle and gas kernels](kernels.py)

- **Nucleation.** A study-specific CUDA kernel samples Poisson births with J = Jmax C/(1 + C), where C is the summed gas-phase precursor-derived intermediate concentration in units of 1 µg m⁻³. Jmax is bounded between 0 and 0.35 cm⁻³ s⁻¹. A birth makes a 3 nm dry organic particle and removes its mass from the gas in proportion to the three source inventories. Empty slots are reserved for births. The simulation checks capacity and limits formation to available mass. This source tests whether a modest number source helps the fit. Manchester reported that seed suppressed observable new-particle formation under these conditions. A fitted J is conditional on the assumed cluster size, growth, survival, and instrument cutoff. [Manchester experimental design](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/22/14147/2022/#section2)
- **Deposition.** Each particle has a wall-loss probability 1 − exp(−kΔt). The rectangular Particula wall kernel uses its current dry radius and density, common eddy coefficient, and the 3 × 2 × 3 m chamber. Deposition removes the entire particle and credits the organic and sulfate wall budgets.
- **Coagulation.** We sample uniformly shuffled disjoint pairs, including vacant slots. For two live particles the Particula transition-regime Brownian kernel gives Kᵢⱼ. Conditional on a pairing, the collision probability is Kᵢⱼ(Nslots−1)Δt/V for that collision substep. A collision sums every species into one particle. Four pairing sweeps per chemistry step keep probabilities below one, a checked condition. Brownian properties are updated for merged particles, with unmerged pair properties held at the start of that chemistry step. No fragmentation in number space is prescribed.

## Which measurements inform the fit?

The loss gives equal weight to six blocks. It averages normalized mass and number trajectory RMSE across runs, the run 24 spectrum RMSE beyond the figure-reading tolerance divided by 40,000 cm⁻³ per decade, modal log-diameter RMSE beyond one model bin, run 18 surface RMSE divided by its measured maximum, and water κ RMSE divided by 0.5. These weights set a numerical tradeoff and are not measurement likelihoods.

1. The first five hours supply 1,258 suspended organic mass observations and 682 count observations. The count operator uses 40–600 nm for DMPS and a nominal 5 nm lower limit for the archived WCPC records. The EDF specifies TSI 3785. The campaign table names a 3786; a hard cut approximates a gradual efficiency curve. A newly born 3 nm particle must grow before contributing to the primary count metric. The number source and detector threshold are consequently confounded. [TSI 3785](https://tsi.com/discontinued-products/water-based-condensation-particle-counter-3785) · [Campaign instrumentation](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/22/14147/2022/#section2)
2. The July 30 ternary run 24 heatmap gives 13 mobility diameter intervals at 21 times. We used 272 of its 273 digitized cells; one had less than half of the pixel neighborhood usable. Simulated bin counts were rebinned by overlap in log diameter and converted to dN/dlog₁₀D per cm³, without normalization to the heatmap integral. The colour-reading allowance is 5,000 cm⁻³ per decade. Independent counters and the heatmap can have different integrated concentrations.
3. The seven EDF modal-size records contain 212 points. We identify the maximum of the number spectrum over 40–600 nm after smoothing with a 1.5-bin Gaussian (model bin ratio 1.12); the loss allows one model bin of modal uncertainty. Run 18 provides 31 DMPS surface-area observations. The 192- and 384-particle optimization stages approximate this operator with total dry surface. The subsequent 768-particle selection and final reporting integrate surface only over 40–600 nm. The geometric-mean diameter line on the run 24 figure was reserved as a descriptive check. It is correlated with the fitted spectrum. [Size targets, extraction scripts and DOIs](size_data/README.md) · [Wang et al., Figure S1](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/22/4149/2022/acp-22-4149-2022-supplement.pdf#page=5)
4. For runs 6 and 15, HTDMA predictions use number-weighted mean organic volume fraction for particles within ±0.10 in natural-log dry diameter of 75, 100 or 150 nm. CCN predictions condition on the observed activation diameter, selecting adjacent log bins within ±0.12 of that diameter. They test effective κ conditional on size; they do not independently predict activation diameter. For each probe, κ = (1−φ)κseed + φκorg. Two shared bounded organic κ parameters correspond to HTDMA and CCN. Seed κ comes from the pre-light records, with the 100 nm anchor used for 150 nm. If fewer than three simulated particles support a reading, its prediction is missing and the search incurs a 0.20 κ penalty. Five-minute simulated inventories are interpolated to sample times. ## What was differentiated?

The conditional first step uses Warp reverse-mode AD for an integrated chemical mass-tendency objective evaluated on frozen gas and particle inventories. A second search varies the 70 rate coefficients, precursor maturation and yield parameters, isoprene diversion, injection doses, wall eddy coefficient, seed GSD, condensation allocation, nucleation strength and the two water parameters. Five unused predecessor parameters remain fixed. At each of eight rounds, nine antithetic Gaussian pairs use common particle random numbers across candidates. Warp AD of their Gaussian log densities estimates the gradient of the *smoothed expected* simulation loss, including discontinuous events and instrument masks. Bounded candidates and three gradient step sizes compete in new full nonlinear simulations. The search used 192 initial particles per run for proposals and 384 for selection. No derivative of a single wall hit, birth, collision or modal-bin change is assumed.

A further PyTorch reverse-mode calculation propagates all 50 product-aging rate coefficients through the matrix exponential of the five family-specific reaction systems over 30 minutes. It starts at the current resolved inventories every 15 minutes. Existing particles and their vapor daughters are allowed to interconvert. The conditional perturbation holds other sources and the population trajectory fixed. Because these family reactions are first order, applying the propagator to summed family inventories and then evaluating total mass gives the same result as summing the individual conditional chemical propagations. The candidate rates compete with the incumbent in the complete stochastic model. Both continuous AD calculations check a directional derivative numerically. These are bounded calibrations of an existing network. Reaching a lower score cannot identify individual chemical reactions uniquely. The numerical checks reuse the same chamber observations with new stochastic realizations.

## How sensitive is the joint refit to bounded parameter changes?

We used a 48-point Latin hypercube (design seed 27182) over all 101 active parameter coordinates of the selected particle-resolved fit. Each coordinate was sampled uniformly over one original optimization step on either side of the refitted value, truncated at its original bounds. The 70 chemical rates used the same rate-dependent step formula as the optimizer, evaluated at the refit. The 15 dose factors varied by up to 0.05 each, seed GSD by 0.14, condensation allocation by 0.23, wall eddy coefficient by 1.8 s⁻¹, nucleation upper scale by 0.10 cm⁻³ s⁻¹, and the two organic κ values by 0.065. The five unused coordinates stayed fixed. These independent local sampling ranges describe a sensitivity experiment. Their probabilities and covariances have no observational calibration. [Bounds for every input](results/perturbations/ranges.csv) · [48 parameter vectors](results/perturbations/draws.csv)

For each vector we replayed all 15 five-hour runs at 768 initial particles per run and 30 s chemistry steps with two random seeds (311 and 312). Each replay used the final observation operators and the instrument size window for surface area. Center and frozen controls used identical simulation settings and random numbers. A prespecified subset (the first 12 draws) was also evaluated at 1,536 initial particles per run, 15 s steps and an independent random seed (313). This checks the combined change in numerical settings. The initial four-pairing-sweep trial stopped at a collision probability of 1.171. We discarded that trial and used 12 pairing sweeps per chemistry step for every draw and both controls. Every accepted simulation applies the original mass, number, probability and capacity checks. We tabulate quantiles within each realization and over the 48 draw-by-draw averages of the two 768-particle scores. Spectrum RMSE and modal-diameter MAE in physical units use the unadjusted observations. The objective uses the digitization and modal tolerances described above. The water score includes a 0.20 κ error for an unsupported reading. They do not express confidence intervals for the physical parameters or predictive coverage of other experiments.

For those same first 12 draws, nine paired control experiments each restored one parameter group to its fitted value, leaving all other groups at their jointly perturbed values. The 108 paired controls used the 768-particle setting and seed 311. We report the median absolute change in each error when restoring each group. Effects depend on the chosen ranges, can include interactions and cannot be added to obtain shares of variance. A positive signed effect means restoring that group improved agreement with the measurements (joint error minus restored error). Group sizes range from one to 50 coordinates. Only the joint draws have a second stochastic realization. Repeating identical draw vectors with a second seed provides a separate check on stochastic variability. [Paired scores](results/perturbations/scores.csv) · [Group effects](results/perturbations/group_effects.csv)
