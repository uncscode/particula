# Manchester chamber observations

These are the authors' processed instrument records for four α-pinene-only chamber experiments. Gordon McFiggans and Aristeidis Voliotis deposited the AMS and VOC data at AERIS/EUROCHAMP. The context records credit the Manchester team in the metadata returned by each DOI. The DOI order and experiment mapping below follow the supplement's Table S1. The portal lists the resources as public level 2 data and displays a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence. The CSVs here are reformatted from EDF v1.0 files whose instrument headers are marked preliminary. The loading, NOₓ and seed columns are transcribed from the article's Table 1. All nominal injected VOC loadings have ±15% uncertainty. The ancillary files differ on some initial conditions. For run 4, the AMS description PDF lists 103 ppb α-pinene and about 18 ppb NOₓ. The AMS EDF header and the separate chamber description agree with the article on 155 ppb α-pinene. The chamber description lists about 15 ppb NOₓ. Run 5 has 19 ppb NOₓ in the AMS description, 35 in the chamber description and 35.8 in the initial gas measurement. The paired primary instrument records are identified here by paper run and date. A mechanistic interpretation of differences in stated initial NOₓ requires resolving these inconsistencies.

## Data products

- `observations.csv` retains the original timestamps and values of every AMS organic mass and VOC fraction sample. Each run has 101 mass values in µg m⁻³ from lights-on to 6 h at 216 s spacing and 301 dimensionless α-pinene fractions from about 1 min to 5 h 1 min at 60 s spacing. Run 1 has a 0.012 s offset. Table 2 lists the GC-MS acquisition time as 1200 s. Minute values are processed concentrations with correlated errors. Run 5's EDF gives a 2012 calendar origin despite an explicit relative lights-on clock, the 2019 run date and the matching sample pattern. Use the numeric elapsed time. Run 5's normalized signal briefly exceeds unity. The CSV preserves it.
- `context.csv` samples temperature (°C), RH (%), gas monitors, particle counts and DMPS mode values nearest a six-minute grid. The time column is the original sample time in seconds since lights-on. Each measurement appears once. Two zero temperature/RH readings in a pair indicate an instrument dropout and are excluded. The EDF gas units are blank. The paper describes the same NO, NO₂, NOₓ and O₃ mixing ratios in ppb. The DMPS total-number channel explicitly reports particles cm⁻³. Units are missing for WCPC and modal diameter. Direct cross-instrument seed surface estimation has additional uncertainty. WCPC and DMPS initial counts for run 1 are 10,500 and 20,699 respectively. No complete seed size spectrum, per-run pressure or sulfate mass time series occurs in the files used here.
- `source_manifest.csv` lists each downloaded EDF used or inspected, including its DOI, direct public source URL and SHA-256 checksum. To recreate both CSVs with the standard Python library, run `python prepare.py --raw-dir /tmp/voliotis-eurochamp` from this directory. Different cache locations are supported. The context gas file for run 4 is available for clock investigation in that cache. Its header gives a 1904 origin, the numeric range 3600–66580 resembles seconds of day, and the record includes pre-experiment chamber cleaning. It is excluded from the relative-time context CSV. All other exported context records use the explicit elapsed clock.

## Which mass is comparable?

The EDF AMS variable is `SOA_mass_conc`, with no embedded wall-correction flag. Its peak values in runs 1, 4, 5 and 6 are 273.06, 68.47, 109.46 and 31.44 µg m⁻³. These match the publication's Table 1 maxima (273.2, 68.6, 109.5 and 31.5) within 0.15 µg m⁻³. The strong late decline and magnitudes match the upper, raw measurement panel of Fig. 3. The lower, particle-wall-loss-corrected panel reaches about 400 µg m⁻³ in full-loading experiments and about 50 µg m⁻³ in the one-third-loading experiment. Compare the archive with modeled suspended organic mass, excluding inorganic seed. The experimenters used nearby size-resolved dark sulfate runs for their correction. Those rates are absent from the paired run records. Vapour-wall losses are unquantified in the published yields. [Voliotis et al., Fig. 3 and Sect. 4](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/22/14147/2022/)

Independent Manchester chamber characterization provides particle removal sensitivities. At 50% RH with mixing, approximate mean decay magnitudes read from Fig. 5 are 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ near 100 nm, 8 × 10⁻⁵ near 200 nm and 6.7 × 10⁻⁵ near 300–400 nm. Size, charge, mixing and bag history affected the measurements. The same facility reports a dark α-pinene loss of (2.24 ± 0.67) × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹. Transferring that rate to illuminated experiments would attribute some VOC depletion to walls. An independent gas budget also has to accommodate the measured ozone. [Shao et al., Sects. 3.3–3.4 and Figs. 5–6](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/15/539/2022/)

## Ozone reaction budget

`ozone_budget.py` integrates the consumption of α-pinene attributable to ozone over the first 18,000 s, the common five-hour window in the calibrated VOC records. For fractional α-pinene remaining $f$, the fraction of the initial loading consumed by ozone is $\int k(T)[O_3](t) f(t)\,dt$. The comparison denominator is the decrease in the measured fraction between 0 and 18,000 s. The calculation uses the native gas monitor data at 10 s, linear interpolation of the gas and processed VOC records, and the union of instrument sample times as quadrature boundaries. The VOC fraction remains at its first recorded value of 1 from lights-on until the first gas chromatograph-derived record at approximately 60 s. Run 1 and 5 use measured temperature, run 6 uses 299 K, and conversion of ppb to molecules cm⁻³ assumes 101325 Pa throughout. Instrument offsets remain in the records, including slightly negative O₃ in run 1. [Paper, Sect. 3.3](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/22/14147/2022/)

Using the paper's rate of 9.6 × 10⁻¹⁷ cm³ molecule⁻¹ s⁻¹ gives integrated ozone shares of **53.5%, 99.5% and 66.2%** in runs 1, 5 and 6. An alternative temperature-dependent evaluation, $k(T)=8.22\times10^{-16}\exp(-640/T)$ in the same units, gives 54.2%, 99.8% and 66.6%. Cox et al. give a reliability of ±0.15 in log₁₀ k at 298 K. Applying that factor alone to run 5's fixed-rate calculation spans 70–140%. A share above one signals inconsistency with the available precursor for those assumptions. These are sensitivity bounds. Unresolved oxidant exposure, monitor calibration and alignment, temperature/pressure and the VOC processing also affect the residual. The measured VOC loss remaining after this integration cannot uniquely identify OH or wall loss. Run 4's conflicting absolute gas clock precludes a matched run 4/5 oxidant attribution. Execute `python ozone_budget.py --raw-dir /tmp/voliotis-eurochamp` after preparing the source cache to recreate `ozone_budget.csv`; this calculation requires NumPy. [Cox et al., Table 1](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/20/13497/2020/)
