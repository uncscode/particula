      How does water uptake change organic aerosol partitioning?

      Section titled “How does water uptake change organic aerosol partitioning?”

      At fixed total organic loading, changing water activity can change both aerosol water and the amount of organic material in the condensed phase. Here a five-component volatility distribution is solved at equilibrium, first at and then across a water-activity sweep. The aim is to interpret that equilibrium sensitivity before adding time-dependent transport.

      Use the BAT phase-property walkthrough to understand the activity coefficients and phase weights. This functional calculation does not require an aerosol container and does not predict a condensation timescale. It uses the reduced thermodynamic framework of Gorkowski, Preston, and Zuend (2019).

      Python · section 1

      Span volatility while fixing the available organic mass

      Section titled “Span volatility while fixing the available organic mass”

      The dry saturation concentrations range from to µg/m³. Total gas-plus-particle organic concentrations are [1, 5, 10, 15, 10] µg/m³, summing to 41 µg/m³. These are independent inputs: saturation concentration describes volatility, while total concentration bounds the available material. All five molar masses are 200 g/mol.

      O:C and estimated density supply the activity calculation. The density helper returns kg/m³ using its default elemental assumptions. The generic components are illustrative, not a measured speciation or fitted emission mixture.

      Python · section 2

      Connect phase properties to mass partitioning

      Section titled “Connect phase properties to mass partitioning”

      At , first calculate organic activity coefficients, water fractions, and alpha/beta phase weights. The partitioning solver then uses them with volatility and total loading to allocate organic mass. The printed organic and water concentrations describe the combined aerosol phases.

      The saved calculation gives about 24.22 µg/m³ condensed organic material and 9.41 µg/m³ aerosol water. Organic mass is bounded by the 41 µg/m³ total; water comes from the imposed equilibrium activity rather than that organic inventory. These values depend on the chosen surrogate mixture.

      Repeat equilibria across water activity

      Section titled “Repeat equilibria across water activity”

      Each of the 50 points from to 0.99 is a separate equilibrium calculation with unchanged organic loading. This is not a time trajectory or a closed water budget. Water activity equals RH/100 only under the assumed flat-interface equilibrium at a common temperature; curvature is omitted.

      The arrays retain condensed mass concentrations, not activity coefficients. Changing humidity influences those masses through both phase properties and partitioning. No parameter fitting to observations occurs in this sweep.

      Interpret the two concentration scales

      Section titled “Interpret the two concentration scales”

      The organic aerosol curve uses the left axis and the water curve uses the right axis for aerosol water, both in µg/m³. Follow each axis separately: a line crossing is not equality of concentrations. The high-activity water response illustrates how strongly water uptake can change while organic mass remains constrained by a fixed inventory.

      The plotted states are equilibrium predictions for these inputs, not evidence of how quickly an ambient aerosol responds or of validated multiphase kinetics.

      Python · section 5

      Use the equilibrium curve as a bounded reference

      Section titled “Use the equilibrium curve as a bounded reference”

      Water activity affects more than aerosol water: it also changes the equilibrium organic partitioning problem. The final checks require finite, nonnegative masses and organic concentration no greater than the available inventory. Those bounds are necessary checks, not proof of solver convergence at every point or agreement with measured aerosol composition.

      For a research extension, vary the total organic loading while preserving its species ratios, then compare the fraction condensed at each water activity. Record solver success and residuals as well as mass bounds; compare the result with a measured humidity-dependent partitioning curve. A time-dependent condensation example would address the separate question of approaching those equilibria.
