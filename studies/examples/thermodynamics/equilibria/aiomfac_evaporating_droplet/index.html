      How long does an evaporating droplet take to reach two-liquid equilibrium?

      Section titled “How long does an evaporating droplet take to reach two-liquid equilibrium?”

      A wet 2-propanol–ammonium sulfate droplet loses water to a finite volume of air. Follow the changing phase amounts and compare the terminal water mass with a standalone equilibrium solve at the same total water inventory. The initial dry mass ratio and temperature match the humidity sweep. Here the gas humidity evolves as water evaporates. A liquid split can occur during a continuous change in particle mass. A second closed-box experiment lets 2-propanol exchange with the gas as well and tracks both vapor inventories.

      Set up a finite reservoir

      Section titled “Set up a finite reservoir”

      One cubic metre contains 10 billion identical droplets at 303.15 K. In each droplet 0.1 ng of dry material comprises 70% alcohol and 30% salt by mass. Only water exchanges with the gas. The initial water mass fraction is 0.61. We choose the total water to place equilibrium near a condensed water mass fraction of 0.43, inside the two-liquid region. A near-zero prescribed surface tension approximates the flat surface of the standalone solver. Check the resulting Kelvin correction before interpreting the kinetic–equilibrium comparison.

      Compare an equilibrium inventory with a transport trajectory

      Section titled “Compare an equilibrium inventory with a transport trajectory”

      Add the gas and particle concentrations before the first step. Passing those totals to the equilibrium solver without an RH closes the reservoir. Native condensation integrates the flux from each trial droplet composition. At each output time, recompute the internal liquid split, reconstruct the combined water budget, and record the gas RH. The diagnostic phase masses partition the same droplet inventory that drives transport.

      Let the alcohol exchange with the gas

      Section titled “Let the alcohol exchange with the gas”

      Start a second box with 20% less particle alcohol and the same water and salt masses. Supply enough vapor to target the same two-liquid endpoint. At 303.15 K the measured pure 2-propanol vapor pressure is 7.859 kPa. The prescribed gas diffusivities are 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s for water and 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s for alcohol. Water and alcohol have independently closed inventories. Their fluxes are coupled through the evolving liquid activities. Ammonium sulfate stays in the droplet. Vapor pressure measurements, Table 4

      Section titled “Identify when the second liquid appears”

      The falling water fraction and rising vapor RH follow the same transfer of water. As the droplet approaches its terminal composition, the organic-rich share grows from zero. Dashed horizontal references come from the closed equilibrium solve, which takes no time steps.
