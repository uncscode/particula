      Which of 1,000 drying droplets first separate into two liquids?

      Section titled “Which of 1,000 drying droplets first separate into two liquids?”

      Two droplets in the same air can have different dissolved compositions. As humidity falls, each loses water at its own rate. Which mixing states develop a second liquid first, and which still contain one liquid at the last observation? This example follows the same 1,000 particle IDs through the ramp. Two coexisting liquids occupy the same droplet and retain its ID.

      Follow the individual inventories

      Section titled “Follow the individual inventories”

      The particles contain water, 2-propanol, sodium chloride and potassium chloride. The three solutes are retained in each droplet while water exchanges with the vapor at 298.15 K. This controlled experiment isolates water-driven liquid separation. Salt crystallization is disabled in this liquid-only calculation. A volatile alcohol can also exchange with the atmosphere. The finite rate of water transfer and the instantaneous equilibrium of up to two internal liquids are separate model assumptions. Activity and phase selection · Liquid coexistence equations

      The random seed is 2012. Each particle begins with 7–13 pg of retained solute, of which 19–36% is salt. KCl supplies 15–70% of that salt by mass. A CPU water-uptake calculation sets the initial 90% RH equilibrium over each curved particle. The GPU independently checks that each state has one stable liquid before advancing the population. The surface tension is 0.072 N/m and the Kelvin density is 1,000 kg/m³. The box holds 1,000 unit-weight particles in 10 cm³ at 101,325 Pa.

      The simulation helper selects ActivityFactory().get_strategy("aiomfac", …) with phase_mode="stable", in water, 2-propanol, NaCl, KCl order. A water-only CondensationSpeciesMap controls vapor exchange. The GPU resident condensation process evolves all particle masses for 0.006 s after each step change in vapor. Each of the 32 imposed RH steps removes 0.25 percentage points, ending at 82% after 0.192 s. Gas water can change again within a step. The plots use the RH from the resulting gas mass. The helper also checks each retained solute and the water budget across particles, vapor and the imposed changes.

      Use the complete example download to obtain the simulation helper. A source checkout also works. Install from the feature checkout using python -m pip install -e "./packages/particula-aiomfac[cuda]" -e ".[plot]". On an NVIDIA GPU, run python example.py --cuda from the extracted ZIP. The calculation returns one row per saved time and one column per particle for water mass and phase count. Every comparison below gives each particle ID the same weight. To save every ID, RH interval and water trajectory in a machine-readable file, run the included helper as python studies/aiomfac-mixing-state/ramp.py --particles 1000 --output ramp.json. The complete download includes the independent CPU solver. Use python studies/aiomfac-mixing-state/equilibrium.py --ramp ramp.json --output equilibrium.json to run its checks on an existing trajectory. The checked numerical data include both calculations.

      Python · section 1

      Locate the first observed split

      Section titled “Locate the first observed split”

      If a droplet changes from one liquid at one observation to two at the next, those observations bracket the event. A droplet already split at the first observation has a left-censored onset in time. A droplet unsplit throughout the record has a right-censored onset in time. The initialization checks that all 1,000 droplets begin with one liquid. The first-split statistic and the fraction currently split answer different questions if a droplet subsequently returns to one liquid.

      Python · section 4

      At what droplet size and humidity does a second liquid appear?

      Section titled “At what droplet size and humidity does a second liquid appear?”

      For each particle, the independent CPU calculation fixes its dry composition and solves for the water amount at which a distinct second liquid can first coexist. It equates water, alcohol and both independent salt chemical potentials across the two liquids and applies the same radius-dependent Kelvin correction. The result is the ambient RH for a completely equilibrated particle of that dry composition and mass. The left panel follows each whole droplet from its initial wet diameter at 90% RH to its wet diameter at first detection of two liquids. The dashed 1:1 line shows where the two diameters would be equal. At a given initial diameter, the vertical distance from the line down to a point is the diameter lost during drying. The short gray segment above each point reaches back to the preceding one-liquid observation. Diameters are volume-equivalent spheres, calculated from the same additive material volumes as the kinetic model, . Color gives the initial salt fraction of the retained dry mass. On the right, compare the equilibrium RH for each dry composition with the two ambient humidities bracketing its kinetic onset.

      What the ramp shows

      Section titled “What the ramp shows”

      All 1,000 particles developed two liquids and remained split through the final sample. First detections occurred in six observations spanning 85.75% to 84.50% RH. The 14 earliest detections followed a one-liquid observation at 86.00% RH. The 25 latest followed one at 84.75% RH. Median salt content was 19.8% of retained mass among those first 14 particles and 35.4% among the last 25. Consecutive observations differ by 0.25 RH percentage points. No onset was censored in this run.

      Initial wet diameters ranged from 3.80 to 4.98 µm. At the first two-liquid observation they spanned 3.46 to 4.48 µm, with a median of 3.99 µm. All 1,000 points lie below the 1:1 line because every droplet had shrunk by the first detection of a second liquid. The median diameter loss was 9.3% of the initial wet diameter. Across the shorter interval from the last one-liquid sample to the first two-liquid sample, the median diameter change was 0.020 µm.

      The independent equilibrium cloud points span 85.13% to 86.21% RH across the same 1,000 dry inventories, with curvature included. Water loss takes time along the imposed ramp. Every kinetic onset interval fell below its particle’s equilibrium cloud point. The median lag was between 0.31 and 0.56 RH percentage points. Both calculations agreed on the number of liquids at all 33,000 sampled particle compositions.

      Among the 250 particles with the least salt (19.0–23.5% of dry mass), the median first detection was 85.50% RH, against a median equilibrium onset of 86.01%. For the 250 with the most salt (31.5–36.0% of dry mass), these values were 84.75% and 85.33% RH. Both the equilibrium and kinetic calculations put the salt-rich group’s median onset at lower humidity. These are calculated thresholds for a liquid-only model with retained alcohol.

      The equilibrium sweep and single evaporating droplet isolate those two calculations for a single starting mixture. A repeat ramp with a longer residence time at each humidity would test the role of kinetic lag. A time-step check kept the same 6 ms plateaus for 16 of the original IDs. Dividing each plateau into two steps moved two of their first detections one saved interval later. Two and four steps gave the same onset brackets for all 16. Their maximum water-mass difference was 0.13%.
