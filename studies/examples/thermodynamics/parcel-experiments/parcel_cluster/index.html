      What crosses a shared parcel face?

      Section titled “What crosses a shared parcel face?”

      A gas follows the prescribed air exchange between two parcels. A droplet can move in the opposite direction if its settling speed exceeds the updraft. Use two experiments to track these mechanisms separately. First move an organic vapor across a face and ask how receiver particulate mass responds to the flow rate. Then release two particles of different sizes above a horizontal face and predict which crosses first.

      These are prescribed-flow experiments. The parcel histories come from Particula, and the settling column has uniform air velocity. The smooth sensitivity and the particle crossing time each have an independent check. The full example requires an NVIDIA GPU for the reverse-mode calculation.

      Python · section 1

      Give each parcel its own forcing record

      Section titled “Give each parcel its own forcing record”

      The donor contains one kilogram of dry air, and the receiver contains two. At equal pressure and temperature their unequal volumes make concentration an unsuitable quantity to sum across the face. Generate separate histories using different updrafts and recording cadences so matching the same record number would also give the wrong exchange time. Track inventories in kg and compare all parcel diagnostics at common physical times in seconds.

      Python · section 2

      Diagnose organic mass and its sensitivity to flow

      Section titled “Diagnose organic mass and its sensitivity to flow”

      Each transfer uses the donor’s current gas concentration. An irreversible first-order uptake of the dilute organic vapor represents transfer to the particle phase. Follow gas and particulate mass together to close each parcel’s budget. Terminal receiver particle mass measures how much of the transported vapor has condensed by the observation time.

      Perturb the flow on both sides of its specified value and repeat the whole experiment. Centered differences check the CUDA reverse pass on the same time mesh. Halving the exchange step in a CPU calculation tests sensitivity to the duration over which donor concentration is held fixed.

      Python · section 3

      Release two particles above a horizontal face

      Section titled “Release two particles above a horizontal face”

      In a separate fixed column, place both particles one centimeter above the interface at m. Use radii of 1 and 10 µm, material density of 1000 kg/m³, and room-temperature air. Compute the terminal speeds with the Stokes–Cunningham settling law and set the updraft halfway between the speeds. The smaller particle rises and the larger one sinks. With constant properties the exact crossing time is the initial distance to the face divided by the large particle’s net downward speed. Check the event time and each particle’s altitude on both CPU and CUDA. Both paths use the same terminal speeds, so this comparison tests the event timing and particle motion conditional on that drag law.

      Python · section 4

      Read flux, response and crossing against the controls

      Section titled “Read flux, response and crossing against the controls”

      Follow the donor loss and receiver gain beside the cumulative face transfer. Only part of the transferred mass has condensed by the end.
