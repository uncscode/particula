      Water and organic uptake in a rising parcel

      Section titled “Water and organic uptake in a rising parcel”

      A warm parcel carries two droplet classes and finite reservoirs of water vapor and 1,4-butanediol. Both gases can change the droplet composition and release latent heat. We lift the parcel 150 m twice. In the second ascent we disable organic exchange while keeping its initial condensed mass and vapor loading. Comparing the two ascents isolates this organic transfer. We then start a separate parcel at 82% relative humidity and follow it 1,500 m through cloud formation, tracking water saturation and activation of each nucleus class. Both ascents are controlled numerical experiments. From the repository root, install both local projects with python -m pip install ./packages/particula-aiomfac ".[aiomfac,plot]", then run python docs/studies/examples/thermodynamics/parcel-experiments/aiomfac_multivapor_parcel.py. The full example runs on a CUDA device with conditional graph support. Pinned analytical mixture

      Define the wet particles and gas reservoirs

      Section titled “Define the wet particles and gas reservoirs”

      Each class starts at the recorded water/vanillic acid/butanediol/NaCl mass fractions. Initial wet radii are 1 and 2.5 µm. The two classes represent 50 million and 20 million droplets in one kg of dry air. The benchmark prescribes additive component volumes with densities of 1000 kg/m³, surface tension of 0.072 N/m, unit accommodation, and continuum diffusivities of 2.11e-5 and 7e-6 m²/s at 273.15 K and 101325 Pa. These diffusivities scale with temperature and inverse pressure.

      The organic reference pressure is 0.73 Pa at 298.4 K from Toktonov, Table B4 (transpiration data from 298.4 to 349.3 K). We continue its measured vaporization enthalpy of 79.01 kJ/mol as a constant over this short ascent, including a small extrapolation below the first data point. The water pressure is prescribed at 3169 Pa at 298.15 K, with constant latent heat 2.5 MJ/kg. The short ascent stays near 297–298 K.
