      When a size-distribution plot hides a composition error

      Section titled “When a size-distribution plot hides a composition error”

      Can a representation that conserves each species during condensation remain suitable after coagulation begins? This example follows organic uptake onto sulfate seeds, then exposes a limitation of fixed-composition binned coagulation. The question matters when interpreting secondary organic aerosol: a plausible size curve does not guarantee a valid species inventory.

      This is a method-boundary demonstration, not a reliable composition forecast. Condensation creates size-dependent composition. The subsequent binned coagulation changes bin populations without transporting donor species into destination bins. Its saved per-species inventory changes range from about −59% to +35%. Those are observed defects, not acceptable tolerances, and a smaller timestep cannot supply the missing species transport.

      Read the coagulation contract. Use a supported resolved representation when collisions must mix size-dependent species composition.

      Prepare the calculation and the comparison

      Section titled “Prepare the calculation and the comparison”

      The imports support native aerosol construction, process execution, and plotting. Keep the full 250-bin grid and 100,000 condensation substeps when reproducing the saved run. The important comparison is between inventories before and after each stage, not merely whether the script finishes.

      Make the illustrative inputs explicit

      Section titled “Make the illustrative inputs explicit”

      Ten organic surrogates have specified molar masses in g/mol, O:C and H:C ratios, and dry saturation concentrations in µg/m³. Density estimates use those elemental descriptors. The initial gas concentrations are multiplied by 10,000; this intentionally concentrated setup is not a typical ambient loading or a validated experimental mixture. The source does not supply traceable material references for every numerical input.

      Saturation concentration characterizes pure-component volatility at the chosen temperature. It does not mean that a mixture is at equilibrium whenever its gas concentration reaches that value: activity and curvature also enter the particle-surface pressure.

      Section titled “Convert saturation concentration to a pressure model”

      Each organic receives a strategy constructed from its saturation concentration and molar mass at 298.15 K. The builder converts µg/m³ and g/mol to consistent pressure inputs.       Align gas and particle species

      Section titled “Align gas and particle species”

      The unified order is sulfate followed by the ten organics. Gas concentrations are converted to kg/m³; temperature and pressure occupy the environment container. Sulfate starts with zero gas concentration and an effectively nonvolatile vapor-pressure input. Its later exclusion from condensation is explicit, rather than inferred from its name.

      Python · section 4

      Construct seeds with a known number measure

      Section titled “Construct seeds with a known number measure”

      The 250 radii span about 32–501 nm. A lognormal PMF assigns number concentration per bin, with nominal total 10¹³ m⁻³, mode radius 100 nm, and geometric standard deviation 1.4. Per-particle sulfate mass comes from spherical volume; organic masses initially vanish. Summing bin concentrations, rather than integrating them as dN/dr, gives the represented number density.

      Ideal molar activity is assumed. Sulfate and organics occupy separate surface phase labels; the low organic surface tension of 0.0003 N/m is an illustrative assumption, not a measured property. These inputs limit the physical interpretation even before the coagulation representation becomes unsuitable.

      Python · section 5

      Identify what each process can change

      Section titled “Identify what each process can change”

      Native discrete ParticleData stores per-bin number density at unit reference volume. Condensation changes species masses and hence radius. The later binned coagulation changes number density at fixed per-bin masses and compositions. This distinction is the central test: after unequal uptake across sizes, reweighting fixed compositions need not conserve individual species.

      Record the reference species inventory

      Section titled “Record the reference species inventory”

      Assemble particles, gas, and environment, then independently sum particle species mass times bin number density and add gas concentration. The result is a species mass density in kg/m³. This reference includes vapor, so transfer between gas and particles should not change it in the closed condensation stage.

      Use the failure to choose a representation

      Section titled “Use the failure to choose a representation”

      Condensation passes the stated closed-species check, but fixed-composition binned coagulation fails to preserve the size-dependent species inventories created by that uptake. Consequently the final composition is not a trustworthy SOA prediction, even though particle number decreases and the plot looks smooth.

      A useful next study would initialize an equivalent particle-resolved population, run the same staged forcing, and compare both size spectra and each species’ gas-plus-particle inventory. Test timestep and sampling convergence separately. Only after the representation passes those checks should measured volatility, surface tension, and loading be used to investigate a particular SOA system.
