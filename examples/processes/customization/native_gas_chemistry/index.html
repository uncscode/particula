      Track a gas reaction through a chamber pulse

      Section titled “Track a gas reaction through a chamber pulse”

      A falling precursor concentration in a chamber can reflect chemical conversion, dilution, or both. Here the gas concentration of product B distinguishes those processes. We inject a known mass of precursor A at 4 s and predict both gas trajectories independently. The two boxes have different volumes and initial concentrations. All inputs are synthetic.

      Write the mechanism once

      Section titled “Write the mechanism once”

      The reaction converts one mole of A to one mole of B at 0.3 s⁻¹. A and B have equal molar masses in this case. The same native Python mapping can be saved to JSON or YAML with mechanism.save(path) and read back with ReactionMechanism.load(path). The file contains chemistry, while the binding below supplies the gas names used in this particular chamber.

      The example download also includes a bimolecular NCAR 1.3.0 source file and its converted native JSON. From the extracted bundle or repository, run python -m particula.dynamics.chemistry.ncar docs/data/gas-chemistry/combine-ncar.yaml combine.json to reproduce that conversion. Both mechanisms bind to user-provided gases.

      Sample before and after the injection

      Section titled “Sample before and after the injection”

      Each chemistry and dilution step lasts 0.25 s. We observe every 0.5 s. The observation at 4 s is taken immediately before injection. The chamber protocol adds 3 and 1 µg/m³ of A to boxes of 1 and 2 m³, respectively. The source ledger therefore records 3 and 2 µg. Dilution acts at 0.05 s⁻¹ on all gases and the ledger retains the cumulative injected mass.

      The injection is an external intervention between two simulation segments. We explicitly take a host snapshot at the boundary, update the gas and ledger, and prepare the post-pulse segment at the same physical time. Chemistry and dilution run on the selected backend throughout each segment. The CUDA run downloads observations and checkpoints for the comparisons.

      Python · section 2

      Between injections, A decays at the sum of the reaction and dilution rates. The A+B inventory loses mass only to dilution. Subtracting the analytical precursor concentration from the total gives an independent product trajectory at all 41 observation times. The inert tracer decays by dilution alone. The checks below also compare each backend with its own restarted run.

      Compare conversion with inventory loss

      Section titled “Compare conversion with inventory loss”

      The vertical line marks the pre-pulse observation. The slope of A steepens with chemical loss while B accumulates from conversion. A+B tracks the dilution-only prediction. The two initial loads and injected masses produce different trajectories even though both boxes use the same rate. Each analytical line can be compared with independently integrated points.

      Interpreting the two boxes

      Section titled “Interpreting the two boxes”

      The faster decline of A relative to total A+B isolates chemical conversion from the known ventilation loss. A measurement of A alone would confound them. The pulse ledger records the mass added at the boundary even after dilution removes most of the injected gas. A next experiment could vary temperature for a bimolecular Arrhenius reaction and use the product trajectory to test the assumed rate law.
