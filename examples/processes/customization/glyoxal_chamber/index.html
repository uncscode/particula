      Can wet aerosol release glyoxal while it forms an imidazole?

      Section titled “Can wet aerosol release glyoxal while it forms an imidazole?”

      Galloway et al. exposed wet ammonium sulfate seed to glyoxal in the dark, then diluted the chamber. Replay the vapor in their Figure 6 and compare two particles with the same initial composition. Both can absorb and release glyoxal. Only one can turn it into 1H-imidazole-2-carboxaldehyde (IC). The gas observations drive the calculation. Particle measurements test it. Chamber study, experiment 3

      Recover the measurements and start with wet seed

      Section titled “Recover the measurements and start with wet seed”

      Table 1 reports 293 K, 56% RH, a calculated 37% liquid water fraction and 87 µm³ of wet seed per cm³ of air. We use this water fraction by volume, additive volumes, 1 atm, and a representative wet radius of 100 nm. Sulfate normalizes the particle measurements, cancelling proportional seed losses. Radius is an assumption. The number of represented particles follows from the measured volume. Figure 6 gas markers specify the forcing in ppbv.

      Give condensation and chemistry distinct material transfers

      Section titled “Give condensation and chemistry distinct material transfers”

      Only kinetic condensation exchanges vapor with the dissolved pool. On this seed the initial salt molality exceeds 12 mol/kg water. Following Kampf et al. and the implementation in Knote et al., cap salting-in at 12, assign a bound/dissolved equilibrium ratio of 0.5 and use relaxation times of 44,000 and 47,000 s initially. Recompute the remaining salt molality as chemistry consumes ammonium sulfate and releases water. Below 12 mol/kg the ratio is one and the two times are 250 and 5500 s. The contribution of residual acid to salting-in is uncharacterized here. Divide total Henry capacity between the pools once. Dissolved masses count glyoxal equivalents, including rapid hydration implicitly. Partitioning measurements · Two-pool parameterization, equations 1–4

      Convert the water-volume Henry law into an effective pressure for the native mole-fraction activity strategy at the beginning of each outer step. As a result the equilibrium surface pressure is dissolved moles divided by water volume and Henry capacity. Match the native condensation flux slope to the measured monomer relaxation time by solving for an effective accommodation coefficient at the current size and composition. This coefficient includes condensed-phase resistance. Use a flat interface limit for this bulk-solution equilibrium comparison.

      The second pathway uses finite ammonium sulfate. Per mole of IC, two glyoxal and one salt formula unit produce IC, three waters and one sulfuric acid equivalent. The acid lane accounts for the sulfate and protons left when two ammonias are consumed. Prescribed pH controls the rate. Acid equivalents do not specify aqueous speciation. At each pH, derive the conditional rate from the published initial rate at 2 M ammonium, carry its activity coefficient as fixed, and extrapolate with the measured second orders in glyoxal and ammonium. Two ammoniums per salt and M-to-SI conversion enter the coefficient. This extrapolation to concentrated aerosol is uncertain. Product kinetics and initial rates

      Replay the chamber and close the forced budget

      Section titled “Replay the chamber and close the forced budget”

      Use linear interpolation between gas measurements. Prescribe the midpoint concentration for each interval, then let the finite gas and particles evolve together. Record the difference at each prescription as an external gas exchange. Reprepare the CUDA sequence at each interval to refresh the effective Henry pressure using the current composition. Every chemistry and condensation step on that backend runs on the GPU. Observation and forcing explicitly cross the device boundary.

      Report organic mass per sulfate mass and IC per initial sulfate. Use the same two 20-minute windows for measured and simulated organic decline, one immediately preceding dilution and one at the end. Retain the raw marker scatter and compare its direction with modeled product accumulation.

      Estimate a reversible response from chamber growth

      Section titled “Estimate a reversible response from chamber growth”

      At dilution the measured organic/sulfate ratio is about 3.07. With the assumed water and sulfate, the literature-based reversible run has reached only 0.65. Even at equilibrium with the measured 174 ppbv of vapor, its capacity would be about 1.47. Its two initial relaxation times are 12 and 13 hours, comparable to the time between seed injection and dilution.

      How well can one reversible reservoir describe this chamber? Let be organic/sulfate and the measured vapor in ppbv. Model the response as , starting with clean seed at . Estimate positive capacity and response time by least squares on particle observations from injection to the dilution line. Each marker has equal weight. Continue from the fitted state with both parameters fixed and the measured vapor as input. Particle observations after dilution enter only the comparison. This empirical calculation describes the total organic response and makes no prediction of IC abundance.

      Compare the release and the persistent product

      Section titled “Compare the release and the persistent product”

      The organic-to-sulfate panel compares dry organic mass with sulfate ion mass. Retain all digitized observations from seed injection onward. The chamber instrument did not supply a calibrated IC mass. Normalize the measured fragment and modeled IC separately to their medians just before dilution so that only the direction and timing can be compared.

      Python · section 5

      What the comparison tells us

      Section titled “What the comparison tells us”

      The chamber organic/sulfate ratio falls from 3.066 to 2.753 in the selected windows, a 10.20% decline. Its uncalibrated nitrogen marker approximately doubles. The chamber fit predicts a 6.7% decline from 3.01 to 2.81. Its 6.9-hour response recovers the direction and part of the magnitude using the growth data alone to set its parameters. Water content, reversible chemical storage and instrument response can all affect the fitted capacity. Both literature-based organic ratios rise across these same windows. The reaction run can release vapor during individual intervals while ending with more total organic mass. The reversible pools are still filling at dilution, and transferring organic carbon into IC also changes how much glyoxal the particle can take up. The IC signal is especially sensitive to the prescribed pH and ammonium activity. A measurement of particle acidity together with a calibrated product abundance would test that extrapolation.
