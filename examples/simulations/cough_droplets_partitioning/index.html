      Audit a respiratory-droplet illustration before using its predictions

      Section titled “Audit a respiratory-droplet illustration before using its predictions”

      An evaporation calculation can conserve mass and still represent the wrong materials. This example follows a multicomponent droplet population in initially 30% RH air, while exposing a necessary earlier step: checking that requested chemical names resolve to the intended species.

      The retained property lookup fails that identity check. In the saved output, “Lysozyme” resolves to hexachlorophene and “IgA” to isoguanine; the requested calcium and magnesium bicarbonates resolve to carbonates. Those are different substances, not acceptable protein or salt surrogates established by this example. Consequently the results illustrate the workflow and its input-audit failure; they must not be interpreted as quantitative respiratory-droplet or exposure predictions.

      Read condensation and evaporation for the mass-transfer mechanism. The useful question here is what additional checks are required before adapting that mechanism to a material mixture.

      Compare requested labels with resolved identities

      Section titled “Compare requested labels with resolved identities”

      The twelve requested labels span water, salts, small organics, and biological materials. The utility first attempts an exact lookup and can fall back to a similar name. Printing the returned name, CAS identifier, and structure is therefore a scientific input check, not merely a status message. The mismatches identified above invalidate the claim that these arrays describe the requested respiratory-fluid composition.

      The independently sampled mass ranges are illustrative and lack a cited composition dataset here. Molar masses are kg/mol, densities kg/m³, surface tensions N/m, and vapor pressures Pa. The NaCl surface-tension override is an assumed constant; it is not a concentration-dependent electrolyte model. A verified identity and a numerical property value are separate requirements.

      Distinguish reproducible inputs from validated inputs

      Section titled “Distinguish reproducible inputs from validated inputs”

      The sample contains 1000 particles at 10⁻⁴ cm⁻³ (100 m⁻³), corresponding to 10 m³ represented volume, at 298.15 K. The fixed sampling seed controls this realization, not the accuracy of the material model.

      Missing vapor pressures are replaced by 10⁻³⁰ Pa, making those lanes nearly nonvolatile. Missing surface tensions receive 0.072 N/m. These substitutions allow execution; they do not resolve missing measurements or incorrect identities. Record them as assumptions before interpreting any partitioning.

      Initialize a finite gas reservoir at 30% saturation

      Section titled “Initialize a finite gas reservoir at 30% saturation”

      Water gas concentration is 0.3 times its modeled saturation concentration; other vapor lanes start near zero. Gas names retain the requested labels even when property lookup returned another substance, so names in a container do not certify the underlying material identity.

      Temperature is fixed at 298.15 K and pressure at 101325 Pa. Humidity is only initialized here. The active loop does not reset it to 30% at each step.

      Python · section 5

      Track what the composition sampling actually constructs

      Section titled “Track what the composition sampling actually constructs”

      The three supplied radius modes are 0.3, 4, and 40 µm with relative weights 15, 2, and 0.2. They define an illustrative sample, not a validated cough-size distribution. Water-equivalent spherical mass is multiplied independently by each sampled composition factor. Those factors are not jointly normalized, so the final total mass and reconstructed radius need not exactly equal the water-only sample from which they were built.

      The κ array is assumed, with water in lane zero. Each species receives its own surface-phase label, so this configuration retains species-specific surface tensions rather than averaging them across one shared liquid phase. It does not calculate physical phase separation. The inventory helper counts weighted particle mass plus gas mass in the represented volume.
