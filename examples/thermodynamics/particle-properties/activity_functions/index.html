      Compare activity rules along a composition path

      Section titled “Compare activity rules along a composition path”

      How much can an assumed mixing rule change the activity assigned to the same mixture? We follow binary and ternary composition paths, comparing mole-, volume-, and mass-fraction rules with a κ water-activity parameterization. This helps separate a change of composition coordinates from a change of thermodynamic model before coupling activity to condensation.

      Every vertical axis is activity, not activity coefficient. In the molar standard-state convention, ; ideal molar mixing has but generally . Substituting mass or volume fraction for activity is a different approximation, not another way to plot the same Raoult-law prediction. These generic components are not a calibrated material system. For construction interfaces, continue with the strategy comparison.

      Python · section 2

      Define the path before interpreting the curves

      Section titled “Define the path before interpreting the curves”

      Water mass increases from 0 to 100 relative units while component 2 remains at 30. In the ternary path, component 3 decreases from 150 to 10. The common mass scale cancels in these rules; total mass is not held constant. Densities are kg/m³ and molar masses are kg/mol.

      All panels use water mole fraction on the horizontal axis, computed once from these same masses. The ternary curves therefore trace one changing composition path, not a full ternary phase diagram. Component 3’s relative abundance changes at the same time as water’s.

      Python · section 3

      Use mole fractions as the reference

      Section titled “Use mole fractions as the reference”

      Ideal molar activity equals mole fraction. Water’s curve therefore lies on in both panels. In the binary panel, the solute follows ; in the ternary panel, the two solutes share that remainder according to the chosen path. This is a reference model, not evidence that the generic mixture is ideal.

      Python · section 4

      Weight by liquid volume

      Section titled “Weight by liquid volume”

      The volume rule normalizes and returns those fractions as activities. Its curves need not lie on the molar reference, even though the horizontal coordinate is unchanged. The assumed additive component volumes and the choice to equate volume fraction with activity are separate modeling assumptions; density conversion alone does not establish thermodynamic accuracy.

      Python · section 5

      Weight by mass

      Section titled “Weight by mass”

      The mass rule normalizes the input masses directly. Because water is much lighter per molecule than either solute, its mass fraction is below its mole fraction along these paths. The curved water trace against is therefore expected. It does not indicate a numerically inconsistent mass conversion.

      Change water’s rule with κ

      Section titled “Change water’s rule with κ”

      The illustrative solutes have κ=0.6 and 1.2; the water lane’s κ is unused. Water activity follows for the solute volumes . Its dry endpoint is zero here. Along the ternary path, the relative contributions of the two solutes also change, so there is no single constant effective solute κ for the entire curve.

      The nonwater lanes retain ideal molar activities. Compare them with the first pair of panels to see that this model changes water’s rule without computing nonideal activity coefficients for every component. The parameterization follows Petters and Kreidenweis (2007); the particular solute parameters here are illustrative.

      Python · section 7

      What survives a change of mixing rule?

      Section titled “What survives a change of mixing rule?”

      The composition path and its mole-fraction coordinate stay fixed, while the predicted activities change. Thus interpreting a condensation calculation requires naming its activity approximation as well as its composition. Agreement at an endpoint or the fact that fractions sum to one does not choose the physically appropriate rule.

      For a research comparison, hold the dry solute ratio fixed, vary only water, and compare predicted with measured water activity at one temperature. Use residuals over the measured range to assess a fitted κ against ideal molar mixing. That controlled path would separate water uptake from the changing dry composition used in the ternary illustration here.
