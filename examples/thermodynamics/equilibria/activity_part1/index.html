      Follow water–organic mixing from composition to water activity

      Section titled “Follow water–organic mixing from composition to water activity”

      Can one water activity correspond to different liquid compositions? The Binary Activity Thermodynamics (BAT) model provides a reduced description of nonideal water–organic mixing. We first inspect composition-dependent activities, then ask for phase properties at prescribed water activities. This connects a composition calculation to the humidity-controlled inputs used in organic partitioning.

      The model and its calibration are described by Gorkowski, Preston, and Zuend (2019). These calculations illustrate its response to idealized molecular descriptors; they are not measurements of a named organic compound or a simulation of phase-separation kinetics.

      Describe one generic organic component

      Section titled “Describe one generic organic component”

      The organic has molar mass 100 g/mol and O:C=0.225. The water-to-organic molar-mass ratio is dimensionless; the density helper takes g/mol and returns kg/m³. Its default elemental assumptions are part of this illustrative density estimate, not independently measured composition.

      Sweep organic mole fraction from 0.001 to 1. The returned arrays contain water and organic activities, mass fractions, and activity coefficients. Keep those quantities distinct: , so an activity coefficient above one is not by itself a phase-separation criterion.

      Python · section 2

      Read activities and coefficients separately

      Section titled “Read activities and coefficients separately”

      The horizontal coordinate is water mole fraction, the complement of the input organic fraction. The first plot shows activities; the second shows activity coefficients. Departures from ideal molar behavior reflect the BAT model’s nonideality for these descriptors.

      The separation helper inspects activity-curve monotonicity to select branches. Nonmonotonic curves can signal a region requiring a phase treatment in this model. Neither an arbitrary activity above one nor a large coefficient alone constitutes experimental proof of liquid–liquid separation.

      Understand the phase-transition weight

      Section titled “Understand the phase-transition weight”

      The helper extracts a separation activity and constructs a smooth transition. This dimensionless parameter weights the water-rich alpha branch relative to the organic-rich beta branch in the reduced model. It is not water mass fraction, a time coordinate, or a measured fraction of droplets.

      The plot shows how that parameter varies with the computed water activity; it does not evolve particles through a separation event.

      Prescribe water activity instead of composition

      Section titled “Prescribe water activity instead of composition”

      Now set O:C=0.25 and ask for properties over to 1. The density and molar mass remain fixed from the preceding setup, isolating the descriptor change rather than updating an entire material-property model. Under flat-interface equilibrium at a common temperature, . Curvature and finite equilibration rates would change that connection.

      The first two returned objects hold phase properties; row 2 contains each phase’s water mass fraction. The beta object may be None when only one branch is used. Plot water fractions alongside to distinguish phase composition from phase weighting. The endpoint near unit activity is limited by the internal composition interpolation, not a pure-water measurement. In the low-O:C panel, the alpha water fraction sits near one while its weight is near zero over most of the plotted range. The helper clamps out-of-branch interpolation to endpoint compositions. Do not read that unweighted branch or the sharp final jump as a resolved physical transition.

      Increase oxygenation while holding other descriptors fixed

      Section titled “Increase oxygenation while holding other descriptors fixed”

      Repeat at O:C=0.6 with the same molar mass, density, and activity grid. In this parameter comparison the higher-oxygenation case uses a single branch, with and no beta curve. Compare that with the lower-O:C panel rather than interpreting the second panel as a later stage of chemical aging.

      Real oxidation can change molar mass, density, volatility, and functionality together. This controlled descriptor change does not represent those coupled processes or establish a universal O:C threshold for every organic aerosol.

      Python · section 6

      Carry the phase information into a research question

      Section titled “Carry the phase information into a research question”

      The model can associate prescribed water activity with different phase compositions and a transition weight. Those are distinct outputs: the amount of water within a phase is not the weight assigned to that phase. The comparison also shows why molecular descriptors matter when selecting a phase treatment.

      A next investigation could vary O:C and molar mass together, record the water activity at which the beta branch disappears, and compare that map with measured phase-separation humidity. Include density sensitivity and the model’s calibration domain. To predict condensed organic mass rather than phase properties alone, continue with liquid–vapor partitioning.
