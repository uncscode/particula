# Fit chamber yields and particle microphysics together

Frame the comparison in the experimenters' terms. Record the run IDs, seed, precursor mixture, gas histories, temperature, RH, light timing, chamber geometry, flow schedule and observation windows that enter each comparison. Identify which inputs were measured, inferred or shared across runs. Measured precursor decay used as forcing makes subsequent SOA predictions conditional on that decay.

## Screen total yield cheaply

Use a bulk mass or low-dimensional volatility model to batch candidate mechanisms and bounded parameter sets across experiments. Calculate mass yield as the increment in organic aerosol mass divided by the mass of precursor consumed on the same concentration basis. Exclude inorganic seed and apply the experiment's starting baseline. For mixtures specify which precursors enter the denominator. When precursor consumption is missing or indistinguishable from zero, fit suspended mass and report the yield as unavailable. Match published wall-correction convention and consumption coordinates within the measured overlap. Preserve raw suspended mass as a separate trajectory for fitting explicit wall loss. For an additivity control, sum the single-precursor aerosol contributions at their respective consumptions before dividing by the stated mixture denominator. [Manchester Figure 5 and Eq. 4](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/22/14147/2022/#section3)

Fit shared chemistry across runs, with dose, forcing and initial seed allowed to vary within experimental bounds. Use bounded global or multistart searches on the fast model, then replay the best and several scientifically distinct near-ties. Inspect mass trajectories as well as peak yields, worst-run errors and mixture residuals. Carry the alternatives, bounds, forcing and observation definitions into the resolved search. For expanding the chemical equations and testing fitted rates use [reaction discovery](../particula-reaction-discovery/SKILL.md).

## Choose microphysics using the available constraints

| Process | Comparison and constraining measurement |
| --- | --- |
| Condensation, evaporation and reactive uptake | Couple shared vapor to the size and composition of individual particles. Test volatility, accommodation, surface-area dependence and condensed aging against growth, volatility or composition measurements. Enforce finite gas supply. |
| Coagulation | Start with a neutral Brownian kernel. Compare charged Brownian for measured or bounded charge states, differential settling for sufficiently different settling speeds, or turbulent shear when dissipation is constrained. Use physically distinct contributions in combinations. Charged Brownian already includes thermal encounters. Coagulation reduces number while conserving the combined species masses of a colliding pair. |
| Particle and vapor wall loss | Compare constant particle loss to diameter-dependent deposition for the chamber geometry. Use representative dark seed decay at several diameters to bound the eddy coefficient after accounting for dilution, coagulation and migration between bins. Treat vapor sorption and possible return separately. Reproduce the authors’ correction, including any assumption about continued growth after deposition. |
| Dilution and mixing | For a well-mixed constant-volume chamber use the measured exchange flow and volume, with concentration tendency `(Q/V)(C_in − C)`. Apply the inlet composition and account for particle outflow as well as gas outflow. Use a tracer to distinguish flushing from wall loss. If injection gradients persist, test a mixing delay or conservative exchange between compartments. Internal exchange conserves the summed volume-weighted inventory. Chamber exchange flow, spatial mixing and near-wall eddy transport have distinct parameters. |
| Nucleation, seed and water | Constrain births with early small-particle counts, growth into the counter window and the seed size distribution. Assign a birth size and debit its material source. For water uptake use the RH or supersaturation and dry size of the actual instrument, distinguishing HTDMA growth from CCN activation. |

Verify the installed representation and CUDA support for the chosen process combination. For a study-specific mixing or chemistry operator, document its equations and test a limiting case before coupling. Use the selected checkout's GPU support matrix and process guides for coagulation, wall loss, dilution, nucleation and resolved chemistry. [Locating version-matched guides](../../references/sources.md)

## Carry the comparison in both directions

1. Initialize particles with the measured seed distribution, sample volume, counts and composition. Transfer the bulk parameter units and reactions. Check a controlled case where summing particle inventories recovers the bulk tendency. Replay the frozen bulk calibrations on the particle model before any refit. This measures the effect of changing representation alone.
2. Fit chemistry and physical parameters jointly to the measurements available for each run. Forward-model the instrument response, including detection cutoffs, wet or dry diameters, sampling times and size bins. Convert bin counts to dN/dlog₁₀D using the bin log width and sampled volume. Keep modal and geometric-mean diameter distinct. Compare mass, counts, spectra, surface area, composition and hygroscopicity where recorded. Use reported measurement errors or declare scales and weights per observable and experiment. Correlated spectral cells and counts derived from them share information.
3. Compare the frozen baseline, chemistry-only, physics-only and joint refits on common runs, seeds, observation windows and scores. Return structured residuals to the fast search. For example, a falling count with stable organic mass favors testing coagulation against deposition with size data. A growing count confined to the detection threshold warrants a growth and detector check before assigning nucleation. Replay candidate changes against both mass yield and size-resolved records. Show tradeoffs when one target improves at another's expense.
4. Jointly perturb rates, yields, uptake, seed, wall, flow and kernel parameters within justified bounds. Vary discrete kernels as separate cases. Compare common-seed differences, repeat selected fits on fresh seeds, and refine time step, collision sampling and particle number. Check species and number budgets including births, mergers, dilution and deposition. Report numerical variability separately from bounded sensitivity. State which experiments were fitted and which observations, if any, test transfer independently.

Deliver the observations and their provenance, selected bulk and resolved parameter sets, comparator scores by experiment and observable, plots of residuals or distributions, and commands for replay. For a literature analysis, identify an archived channel or a controlled simulation that can distinguish competing explanations or bound the effect of a missing measurement. If proposing a correction, compare it with the original treatment across the available runs and report the tradeoffs. The Manchester example pairs matched-consumption yield with size and water records and shows why improving modal diameter can cost agreement in organic mass.
