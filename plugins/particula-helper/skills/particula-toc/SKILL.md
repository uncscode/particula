# Design a TOC figure

Read the final report title and the result it rests on. Together, the title and image should give a browsing reader a reason to open the report. Let the title name the finding. Give the eye something the words alone cannot show.

## Choose the image

- Write a private sketch brief naming the subject, the comparison and the direction supported by the evidence. Identify what remains unknown before choosing arrows, geometry or motion that could imply a mechanism.
- Sketch one recognizable subject cue and one relationship. Size, spacing, overlap, density or the separation of two marks can show what grows, disappears or diverges. Keep comparable quantities on a common visual basis. Use strong silhouettes, empty space and a small palette. Support important color differences with shape, position or line style.
- Use a few direct labels when they help readers identify the subject or compare marks. Typically use fewer than five visible words in total across the figure. Place words beside what they name. Use the remaining space for meaningful details such as the ion pair and beam in the CLOUD study. Keep the headline, explanatory sentences and precise values in the report.
- For example, the CLOUD title names the underpredicted humidity response. Two marks starting together and ending at different heights inside a humidity motif convey the gap. A drawing of ions sticking to a wall would imply a cause the measurements leave open.

## Export and check

For Particula, produce a **3 × 3 inch square**. Use a self-contained SVG with equal physical dimensions (for example, `width="3in" height="3in" viewBox="0 0 300 300"`), or a **900 × 900 pixel** raster with **300 pixels/inch** set on both axes. Preserve crisp edges and generous margins. Keep the detailed quantitative figure in the report.

Place the artwork beside or directly below the report title. Supply concise alt text conveying the relationship; for standalone SVG include an accessible title and description. Inspect the 3 × 3 inch export and a **2 × 2 inch proof** beside the actual report title. At 300 pixels/inch the proof is 600 × 600 pixels. Display or print it at two inches across to judge readability. Check that labels, contextual details and the main relationship remain distinct in grayscale. Can you recognize the topic, see the direction and distinguish the two sides of the comparison at a glance? If the title already supplies a detail, try removing it from the image.
