# Equilibrium κ-Köhler critical dry diameter

Start with a CPU particle property that returns the minimum **dry** diameter whose equilibrium saturation barrier is at or below a prescribed water saturation ratio. Qualify it against the forward Köhler curve before migrating the Antarctic CCN and Mt. Soledad study calculations. At κ = 0.3, 0.2% supersaturation and a diameter-basis Kelvin length of 2.1 nm, the independently calculated threshold is **104.53745312 nm**. [Issue #1744](https://github.com/uncscode/particula/issues/1744)

The original issue covers the CPU property. The proposed extension makes the same diagnostic available on CUDA, on saved states and as a scheduled simulation output, following the [optics observer](../docs/guide/optics/index.md#record-an-optical-history-during-a-simulation).

Research used upstream `7a031857f0`, source version 0.4.0, 28 November 2026. The issue's comparison was made at `bf91687e52`. The checks below used Python 3.12.12, NumPy 2.3.4 and SciPy 1.16.2 on CPU.

## Implementation and qualification record

The public CPU function and float64 Warp device function solve the full equilibrium equation. The [theory](../docs/theory/thermodynamics/kappa-kohler-activation.md) connects the dry cutoff to water activity, the Kelvin length and population weights. The [numerical tests](../particula/particles/properties/tests/kappa_kohler_test.py) refine an independently computed forward wet-diameter peak. The [device tests](../particula/gpu/properties/tests/kappa_kohler_device_test.py) run on Warp CPU and native CUDA, including near-saturation, multiple stationary points and out-of-range results.

- **Run or replay.** The [worked example](../docs/studies/examples/thermodynamics/activity/equilibrium_ccn_spectrum.py) follows dilution of five classes on NumPy and an RTX 5060. Both backends give a 104.53745 nm threshold at 0.2% supersaturation throughout. CCN concentration at that setting falls from 100 to 50 cm⁻³ in 10 s. All 21 samples agree, and replay of three sparse native analysis rows recovers an additional 0.3% setting. The saved example reports synchronized optics-only and joint timings plus archive size for this workload.
- **Passive observation.** The [integration tests](../particula/integration_tests/ccn_observer_test.py) evaluate separate optical and CCN schedules, on-device sampling with snapshots forbidden, equality of stochastic continuation, equal-time restart boundaries, bounded output, archive readback and temperature forcing across two boxes. Dry-volume weighting and tie behavior are also checked [against hand counts](../particula/particles/properties/tests/ccn_test.py). Discrete and resolved native counts are supported. Continuous PDFs require a separate cutoff quadrature.
- **Study migration.** On the 450-entry Antarctic threshold array, the CPU calculation took a median 0.57 ms versus 1.02 ms for the legacy search in a local comparison of five sets of 30 calls. All 1,865 hourly-setting concentrations agreed within 8 × 10⁻¹³ cm⁻³. The Mt. Soledad hard response can shift when a tiny cutoff change crosses an instrument channel. The largest apparent midpoint shift was 0.331 nm with a hard cutoff, and 0.000767 nm with its calibrated smooth response. All five published plot tables matched the field replay byte-for-byte. The reproduction guides document the comparison and pinned inputs. [Antarctic reproduction](../scripts/antarctic_ccn/README.md) · [Mt. Soledad reproduction](../studies/mt-soledad/README.md)

## Public contract

Import the proposed function with `from particula.particles import get_kappa_kohler_critical_dry_diameter`. Place the implementation in `particula/particles/properties/kappa_kohler.py` and export it beside the [Kelvin helpers](../particula/particles/__init__.py). A call with `kappa=0.3, saturation_ratio=1.002, kelvin_diameter=2.1e-9` returns approximately `1.0453745312e-7` m.

| Input | Meaning and domain |
| --- | --- |
| `kappa` | Finite, dimensionless effective dry-material hygroscopicity, ≥ 0. Constant during growth. |
| `saturation_ratio` | Finite water vapor pressure divided by saturation vapor pressure over flat pure water, strictly > 1. A counter setting of 0.2% gives 1.002. |
| `kelvin_diameter` | Finite positive diameter-basis Kelvin length in metres, $A = 4σM_w/(RTρ_w)$. With Particula, use `2 * get_kelvin_radius(...)` in metres. Surface tension is constant during growth. |

Use NumPy elementwise broadcasting across all three arguments. Return a Python float for a scalar result and a float64 array of the broadcast shape otherwise, including empty arrays. Validate the whole input, raising `ValueError` for nonfinite values, domain errors or incompatible shapes. Specify an explicit numerical failure for a mathematically positive result outside float64 range. Treat ratio inputs rounded to exactly 1 as outside the domain. The function takes material properties directly. The existing [κ activity helper](../particula/particles/properties/activity_module.py) computes activity from species inventories, and the [Kelvin term](../particula/particles/properties/kelvin_effect_module.py) has its own species-axis and exponential clipping conventions.

## Equation and numerical method

For wet diameter $D>D_d$, the forward equilibrium ratio is

$$
S_{eq}(D;D_d)=\frac{D^3-D_d^3}{D^3-D_d^3+\kappa D_d^3}\exp(A/D).
$$

At the critical dry diameter, the global peak of this curve equals the supplied ratio. At κ = 0 the peak occurs in the limit of a vanishing water film, giving the exact wettable-insoluble threshold $D_d=A/\ln S$. The full equation applies throughout the proposed domain. The dilute, large-growth approximation in Eq. 10 is useful as an asymptotic check. [Petters and Kreidenweis (2007), Eqs. 6 and 10](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/7/1961/2007/)

For κ > 0, a candidate implementation can find the global threshold with a few scalar brackets per element and no prescribed wet-size grid. The following reduction is derived from Eq. 6. Write $L=\ln S$ and $t=A/D-L>0$. At equality the possible dry diameters are

$$
d(t)=\frac{A}{L+t}\left(1+\frac{\kappa}{\operatorname{expm1}(t)}\right)^{-1/3}.
$$

Increasing the dry diameter at fixed wet diameter lowers water activity. Once the dry diameter exceeds the largest value attained by $d(t)$, the barrier lies below the target at every wet diameter. Thus take the **largest** stationary value. Both endpoint limits of $d(t)$ are zero. Setting the derivative to zero gives

$$
F(t)=3(1-e^{-t})\left(1+\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(t)}{\kappa}\right)-(L+t)=0.
$$

Use `log1p` and `expm1` or scaled log-domain equivalents for small increments and extreme positive κ. Isolate all candidate roots by splitting at the positive turning points of $F$. Setting $F'=0$ gives $3z^2-\kappa z+3(\kappa-1)=0$ for $z=e^t>1$. Include a turning point that is itself a root. Expand the last bracket until the sign is established. Choose scale-relative stopping criteria (a default absolute tolerance in $t$ can exceed the entire near-saturation root). Evaluate all candidates, apply $A$ as a final scale, and handle κ = 0 separately. Compare a vectorized bracketed implementation to the Antarctic workload before selecting the final implementation.

**Why the global maximum matters.** The issue admits every finite κ ≥ 0. A mathematical stress case at κ = 1000, $L=0.5$, $A=2.1$ nm has two local maxima of 0.18500408 and 0.22672743 nm. Use the larger. This checks solver branch selection outside atmospheric conditions. A separate failure appears near saturation even at ordinary κ. For example, at κ = 0.3 and `S = np.nextafter(1.0, 2.0)`, the forward calculation gives about 45.27 m, while the Antarctic and default Mt. Soledad bounds give about 3.37 m and 15.1 µm. Use that case as a numerical limit test.

## Evidence and qualification

The issue grid uses $A=2.1$ nm, κ in {0, 0.01, 0.1, 0.3, 0.6, 1}, and supersaturation in {0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1}%. For each positive κ, independently maximize the **forward** log saturation for a trial dry diameter, then solve for the log dry diameter with a bracketed root finder. For this research check, let $v=(D-D_d)/D_d$ and evaluate $A/[D_d(1+v)]-\operatorname{log1p}[\kappa/(v(3+3v+v^2))]$. Scan 401 equally spaced values of `log(v)` from −50 to 50, refine each detected peak with bounded scalar minimization of its negative, and retain the largest. The outer solve used `log(A/L) + [-30, 1]` in consistent diameter units. Use the analytic value at κ = 0. These finite scan bounds describe the research calculation for the reported fixtures.

| Study method | Largest relative dry-diameter difference from the forward calculation, 24 cases |
| --- | ---: |
| Antarctic growth-factor search, 70 iterations | 3.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ |
| Mt. Soledad, 1,537 water-volume nodes (default) | 6.75 × 10⁻⁶ |
| Mt. Soledad, 3,073 nodes | 2.45 × 10⁻⁶ |
| Mt. Soledad, 6,145 nodes | 5.56 × 10⁻⁷ |
| Mt. Soledad, 12,289 nodes | 1.41 × 10⁻⁷ |

The κ = 0.3, 0.2% case gives 104.53745312 nm in the Antarctic calculation and 104.53703289 nm on the default Mt. Soledad grid. Grid refinement and forward closure measure numerical accuracy for the assumed material. Field uncertainties and instrument response belong to the individual studies.

## Implementation and acceptance

1. **Qualify the CPU property.** Add `particula/particles/properties/tests/kappa_kohler_test.py` with an independent forward peak and round-trip tests. Widen and refine its search until reference diameters and peaks stabilize. For the 24 cases, target relative diameter error ≤ 10⁻⁹ and relative error in **log saturation** ≤ 10⁻⁹ after confirming the reference is at least ten times more precise. Use zero absolute tolerance or a justified scale-aware floor. Checking raw saturation near one can hide a lost supersaturation. Also perturb the dry diameter above and below threshold and verify the direction of the forward response. Test the Kelvin limit, approach from small positive κ, decreasing diameter with κ and S, exact linear scaling with A, and the radius-to-diameter factor of two. Exercise scalar, mixed-zero, empty, incompatible and multidimensional broadcast inputs and all invalid domains. Include the near-saturation and multiple-peak fixtures above, with separately justified extreme-case tolerances and explicit failure if numerical bracketing cannot be verified. Run the new suite and [adjacent Kelvin tests](../particula/particles/properties/tests/kelvin_effect_test.py) using `pytest -Werror --no-cov`.
2. **Migrate the Antarctic constant-κ path.** [`critical_k`](../scripts/antarctic_ccn/model.py) appends a settings axis to an arbitrary-shaped κ array. Preserve that contract with `k[..., None]`, `1 + ss / 100` and `kelvin * 1e-9`, converting metres back to nm once. Test the actual `(anchor, growth_factor, setting)` result with unequal axis lengths. Compare the constant-κ threshold surface and derived counts from the saved inputs at the old and new revisions. Run [the study tests](../scripts/tests/antarctic_ccn_test.py) and the nominal calculation in the [reproduction guide](../scripts/antarctic_ccn/README.md). The paper approximation, salt activity tables and finite-film peaks implement distinct physics.
3. **Migrate Mt. Migrate the two callers that explicitly request 3,073 nodes, including the [film preparation κ inversion](../studies/mt-soledad/source/field-mixtures/films/prepare.py). Separately compare the field midpoint, width and recovered κ on actual study inputs. Record any consequential change to published precision before regenerating outputs. Explain what a user must add to predict instrument counts: inlet hydration, kinetic growth, residence time, activation distribution and optical response. Verify the public import, run the two study compatibility tests explicitly as well as the property tests, and check documentation links. ## Simulation analysis during and after a run

**Support both through one observable.** Start with a callable for a saved state or selected material properties. Then give the same calculation an observer on a CPU simulation and a prepared CUDA simulation. The observer samples after accepted physical steps, on an independently selected output schedule, as optics does. A user can attach it alongside condensation or coagulation and read the latest sampled value or a time history. It needs no position in the pipe of state-changing processes. Existing optics has specific hooks in [the CPU runner](../particula/execution/recording.py) and [the prepared runner](../particula/execution/prepared_recording.py). Add an explicit route for this observer in both runners and allow simultaneous optics and CCN output. [Optical history and observer](../particula/execution/optical_recording.py)

| Scientific question | Where to calculate | What to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What threshold corresponds to a fixed κ, saturation ratio and Kelvin length? | Evaluate once. All three inputs are prescribed, so the threshold is constant even when the population grows. | The threshold in metres and the three inputs. |
| What value did the evolving state have at a few saved times, or at a new set of counter settings? | Evaluate after the run. Use the same configuration and state evaluator used by the live observer. | Sample times, settings and inputs needed to reconstruct κ and A. A new setting can be evaluated wherever those inputs were saved. |
| How does equilibrium CCN number change throughout a run? | Sample live at selected accepted steps, especially when particles are already on the GPU. | A compact history by time, box and saturation setting. Keep occasional full snapshots for independent replay. |

**Define each output before exposing it.** For prescribed common κ and Kelvin length per box, return a critical dry diameter in metres per box and saturation setting. Accept a vector of prescribed water saturation ratios so the same population can be evaluated at several counter settings. If temperature varies, allow A to be calculated from the sampled box temperature and explicitly chosen water properties. Record whether each input is prescribed or sampled. The relative humidity of the simulation can be below saturation while evaluating a hypothetical supersaturated counter setting.

For an evolving population, propose a second, opt-in observable: **equilibrium CCN number concentration** in m⁻³. For each occupied slot use dry volume computed from its non-water material masses and densities. Compare the resulting dry volume-equivalent diameter with its own critical dry diameter using an inclusive threshold. Support prescribed κ first. For composition-dependent κ, require explicit dry-material κ values, species order and water mapping, then volume-weight κ over dry material at observation time. Varying slot κ implies a distribution of thresholds. Expose the bulk number by setting, with per-slot diameters available in an explicit offline evaluation. Omit empty slots and particles with zero dry volume from dry-seed counts. For native 0.4.0 discrete and resolved populations, sum qualifying represented counts per box and divide by the contemporaneous sample volume once. A continuous radius PDF requires a documented quadrature and cutoff treatment on a grid with known dry-size meaning before support is advertised. The observable predicts equilibrium eligibility under the selected conditions. A measured CCN count additionally depends on growth time and the instrument response.

**Post-processing input contract.** A saved native snapshot supplies the particle state. For analysis histories of discrete or resolved particles, require slot material masses, represented populations and sampled volume, plus ordered material densities and water mapping for the dry-volume calculation. Require temperature history only when A is derived from it. Persist κ configuration, saturation ratios, Kelvin convention, units and axes with the output. Validate missing fields on readback. Aggregate number and bulk mass alone cannot recover the fraction above a size threshold. [Recorder variables and population units](../docs/guide/getting-started/recording-restart.md)

### Delivery and checks for the extension

1. **Standalone state evaluation.** Add the CPU state evaluator and demonstrate it on one saved multibox state. Use hand-counted particles below, at and above the cutoff, unequal slot weights and unequal box volumes. Add water to a fixture at fixed dry composition and verify its predicted threshold and eligibility stay fixed. Change dry composition or the imposed setting in a second fixture and verify the predicted response. Verify offline re-evaluation at a setting omitted from the original output.
2. **Device calculation.** Implement the threshold as a float64 Warp device function, with batched evaluation and device-side population reduction for the bulk output. Precompute prescribed, constant thresholds per box/setting when possible. For state-derived inputs, evaluate on the owning device using its current arrays. Check the analytic limit, global maximum, independent forward closure, domain failures and near-saturation fixtures on Warp CPU and native CUDA. Define handling of ties near the threshold and expose failed evaluations before accepting a history batch. Establish a supported range from the direct numerical checks. CUDA support requires passing on actual CUDA hardware.
3. **Scheduled observation and examples.** Give the CPU and prepared runners matching initial, sparse and per-accepted-step sampling behavior within their respective scheduling rules. Prepared sample times must fall on the owner's step grid. Preallocate bounded device workspace, batch-transfer compact rows and status, and make the latest completed sample available on explicit read or flush. Publish an example that records the equilibrium CCN spectrum alongside optics and obtains matching values by post-processing sparse snapshots. Compare passive runs at the same step schedule, including final physical state and stochastic continuation where applicable. Test independent optical and CCN schedules, duplicate-time handling across continuation, units and history readback, and no full-state download during resident sampling. Report native CUDA parity against CPU, compact output size, synchronized overhead and snapshot cadence for that example.
