# Wavelength-dependent shortwave radiative transfer in Particula optics

## Outcome

A researcher should be able to place the same aerosol parcel above an ocean or a dusty land surface, specify a location and UTC time, and obtain the change in reflected sunlight, surface energy uptake, and atmospheric absorption in W/m². The same calculation should accept a vertically resolved column and report which wavelengths contribute to the effect.

Ship **one versioned, idealized Earth reference** that works offline at any latitude and longitude. Location and time determine solar geometry. The reference supplies a prescribed clear atmosphere and solar spectrum. The user selects the surface and perturbs a named input such as aerosol loading. A user who needs actual conditions can explicitly download a selected product or supply local files and replace individual inputs. Report which fields came from the reference, the user, and each product in every result.

## Existing code and proposed ownership

The native CPU optics path evaluates an optical configuration on particle state and returns bulk coefficients by box and wavelength. The column helper already integrates extinction along explicit paths. The Bai limonene study also has a scalar adding–doubling solver. It propagates direct and diffuse radiation through distinct layers, then reduces the outgoing angles to hemispheric quantities. Its station surface sunlight was imposed above an additional modeled cloud. Reuse the transport mathematics and archived cases with that boundary identified. ## 1. Define the calculation and its units

- **Geometry.** One parcel represents one horizontally uniform slab with a caller-specified vertical thickness in metres. A column uses strictly ordered interface heights and an explicit mapping of snapshots or boxes to layers. A representative volume converts particle counts to number density. Inferring a simultaneous vertical profile from a parcel trajectory requires an explicit reconstruction assumption.
- **Layer properties.** Use increasing vacuum wavelengths in metres and arrays shaped `(layer, wavelength)`. Form vertical optical depth from extinction in m⁻¹ times thickness in m. Combine extinction and scattering additively. Weight normalized phase moments by scattering optical depth and preserve the existing zero-scattering validity semantics. Let callers supply measured bulk optics as well as Mie-derived optics. For directional dust studies, this permits measured or nonspherical phase functions.
- **Boundaries.** Identify the top and bottom interface altitudes. Support a complete atmosphere illuminated at the top of atmosphere (TOA) and a bounded slab with prescribed direct and diffuse illumination at its upper face. Specify the diffuse angular distribution. Direct-normal input projects onto the horizontal plane once using the cosine of solar zenith. Carry the spectrum, angular convention, surface, and interval or timestamp into the result.
- **Outputs.** Return direct-down, diffuse-down, and upwelling hemispheric spectral flux at each interface, with dimensions `(interface, wavelength)`. Use W m⁻² m⁻¹ internally and label conversion to W m⁻² nm⁻¹ for plotting. Integrate spectra over declared intervals to obtain W/m². For gas bands treated by quadrature, retain their edges, weights, and band-integrated output. Sub-band monochromatic values require a spectrally resolved calculation.
- **Paired effects.** Define net downward flux as direct-down plus diffuse-down minus upwelling. Define Δ as case minus reference at the same interface and on the same spectral or band grid. Layer absorption equals net flux at its top minus net flux at its bottom. Report absolute fluxes alongside Δ, including reflected flux at the model top and net uptake at the ground when present. For a daily mean, integrate over the full day including darkness.

**First acceptance case.** One homogeneous layer and the same column subdivided into identical layers give the same boundary fluxes within numerical tolerance. An identical case/reference pair gives zero at every wavelength and interface. A black absorbing slab under a direct beam reproduces the slant-path Beer–Lambert solution. The sum of atmospheric and surface absorption plus outgoing TOA flux closes the incident energy budget.

## 2. Build one offline Earth reference

Use a compact extraterrestrial spectral irradiance table at 1 au, a declared clear-sky pressure, temperature, water vapor, and ozone profile, molecular Rayleigh scattering, and a documented gas absorption treatment. Start with zero background aerosol so the supplied Particula population has an unambiguous role. Publish reference coefficients, units, redistribution terms, wavelength coverage, source revisions, and the binning procedure. Select a compact solar reference derived from TSIS-1 and verify the energy in each retained band against its source. Qualify a representative-wavelength or correlated-k gas treatment against a spectrally refined calculation before publishing broadband or satellite-band accuracy. [TSIS-1 reference spectrum and revisions](https://lasp.colorado.edu/lisird/data/tsis1_hsrs/) · [libRadtran spectral parameterizations](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1647/2016/)

Accept latitude, east-positive longitude, elevation, and timezone-aware time. Evaluate solar zenith and azimuth and apply the inverse-square distance correction to the reference spectrum. Clip the atmospheric column at the requested surface elevation using the documented profile interpolation. Use an offline solar-position implementation and cross-check against pvlib's solar position algorithm. Treat a Sun below the horizon as zero incident solar flux in this daylight model. Publish a low-Sun accuracy limit for plane-parallel transfer from comparisons with a pseudo-spherical solver. Exercise polar day and night, both hemispheres, the date line, equivalent UTC offsets, and sunrise/sunset. [pvlib solar position](https://pvlib-python.readthedocs.io/en/stable/reference/generated/pvlib.solarposition.get_solarposition.html)

**Acceptance.** With networking disabled and an empty user cache, a minimal installation can run a selected location and date over either idealized surface. The result identifies the Earth reference version, spectrum, atmosphere, surface parameters, and geometry. The same inputs reproduce the same outputs.

## 3. Implement spectral transport and surface reflection

Start with a NumPy CPU implementation of layer adding using the Bai solver as a prototype. Retain direct, diffuse, and multiple surface–atmosphere reflections. Recover internal interface fluxes, which the archived solver's final hemispheric reduction does not expose. Specify the phase-moment normalization and angular order. When only asymmetry is supplied, expose the selected phase-function approximation and test its effect. Track true unscattered transmission separately if a forward-scattering correction reassigns flux to a numerical direct beam. Refine vertical layers, angular streams, phase moments, and spectral sampling independently. Use a separately run multi-stream DISORT calculation in libRadtran as the numerical reference. [Bai transfer implementation](../studies/bai-limonene/model/seasonal/radiation/transfer.cpp) · [libRadtran solvers](https://www.libradtran.org/doku.php?id=documentation)

| Surface | Offline flux calculation | Directional or observation-constrained calculation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ocean | Prescribed idealized spectral albedo with a documented valid wavelength interval. | Add Fresnel reflection from wind-roughened facets using Cox–Munk slopes. Declare wind magnitude and directional convention. In bands where relevant, supply water-leaving radiance and document whitecap assumptions. Exercise glint and off-glint views. [Cox and Munk](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSA.44.000838) |
| Dusty ground | Prescribed idealized dry mineral-soil reflectance spectrum with Lambertian reflection. Allow a measured spectrum and record its measurement geometry. Deposited dust changes the lower boundary. Airborne dust changes the corresponding layer. | Support land bidirectional reflectance distribution function (BRDF) parameters, initially the MODIS kernel model, with band-specific quality and coverage. Additional soil, vegetation, or snow spectra can enter through the same surface interface. [USGS Spectral Library](https://www.usgs.gov/labs/spectroscopy-lab/usgs-spectral-library) · [MODIS land BRDF](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/science-domain/brdf-albedo-and-nbar/) |

**Acceptance.** Test transparent atmosphere over a Lambertian reflector, a black lower boundary, conservative scattering, pure absorption, and repeated reflections over bright ground. Numerically integrate a directional BRDF over outgoing solid angle with its cosine weight and compare with its hemispheric reflectance under the same incident field. Compare both absolute and paired fluxes against DISORT across aerosol loading, absorption, solar zenith, and surface brightness. Before production comparisons, set absolute W/m² error budgets for Δ from the smallest intended signal. For a study targeting a 1 W/m² difference, a proposed qualification target is 0.1 W/m² absolute numerical error in Δ over its declared test domain. Record failures and the qualified domain.

Validate units, coordinate order, averaging period, band edges, masks, and spatial coverage. Missing or out-of-domain data should identify the affected field so the user can choose a replacement.

## 5. Validate against satellite observations

| Validation target | Comparison and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| **Broadband W/m²** | Start with clear-sky daytime CERES Single Scanner Footprint outgoing TOA shortwave flux over ocean and dry land. Use actual overpass time, footprint, atmospheric profile, aerosol constraints, and surface mix. Quantify spectral energy outside the modeled interval before comparing with the CERES broadband product. Solve land/water subcolumns separately and weight their outgoing flux by footprint contribution. Record cloud screening, uncertainties, and sensitivity to residual cloud. CERES converts directional radiances to flux using scene-specific angular distribution models. [CERES footprint products](https://ceres.larc.nasa.gov/data/) · [Angular distribution models](https://ceres.larc.nasa.gov/data/angular-distribution-models/) |
| **Wavelength dependence and viewing angle** | Add a directional scalar solver with solar zenith, view zenith, and relative azimuth. Qualify its directional radiance against DISORT before comparing with MODIS or VIIRS Level-1B reflective solar bands. Integrate over the sensor's relative spectral response using its published radiance or reflectance convention. Collocate geolocation, geometry, cloud mask, time, spectral response version, and quality flags. Compare at least two bands over both surfaces and stratify ocean cases by glint angle. Check sensitivity to polarization and dust phase function before assigning angular residuals to surface reflectance. [MODIS spectral responses and irradiance calibration](https://mcst.gsfc.nasa.gov/calibration/parameters) · [libRadtran radiance and polarization capabilities](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1647/2016/) |

Select independent overpasses for evaluation. Record which products constrained inputs and any shared instrument lineage. Report sample counts, bias, RMSE, uncertainty, and residuals by surface, wavelength, solar/view angle, and aerosol optical depth. Preserve excluded cases and reasons. A satellite observes one scene per overpass. Compare its absolute flux or radiance directly. Evaluate modeled Δ against a matched observational contrast only when its reference scene and uncertainty can be justified.

## Automated tests

Add deterministic CPU unit tests in `particula/optics/tests/` alongside each implementation. Use analytical values or compact reference values generated independently of the code under test.

| Component | Regression to catch and expected result |
| --- | --- |
| Solar geometry and spectral units | A beam at 60° zenith delivers half its direct-normal irradiance to a horizontal plane. The integrated energy of a constant spectrum agrees in metre and nanometre coordinates. Check polar night and date-line cases against the reference in section 2. |
| Layers and transfer | Check per-wavelength direct transmission against the absorbing-slab solution at oblique incidence. Check zero atmospheric absorption in a conservative scattering column and unchanged boundary flux after splitting a homogeneous layer. Reject mismatched wavelength grids and reversed interfaces. |
| Surface and sensor response | For an atmosphere of zero optical depth, a Lambertian surface reflects its prescribed fraction at each wavelength. Its directional radiance integrates back to its upward flux. Use a constant radiance and a known two-band spectrum to test sensor response normalization and band order. |

Add `particula/integration_tests/spectral_radiative_transfer_test.py`. Exercise the public CPU path from native particle state through population optics, layer geometry, reference sunlight, surface reflection, and paired diagnostics. Run aerosol-present and aerosol-free columns over each ideal surface with networking disabled. Test the one-parcel slab and an equivalent split-layer column. Assert matching outer-interface fluxes, energy closure for each run, and the sign and magnitude of selected spectral and broadband differences against pinned independent solver values. Record the reference configuration and absolute tolerances with the fixture. For the optional reader, replay a compact local input and verify that its boundary units and provenance reach the result. Keep acquisition and the overpass analysis reproducible as separate study runs.

## Implementation and evidence

The CPU implementation, offline Earth reference, and local-file readers follow the module boundaries above. The [optics guide](../docs/guide/optics/index.md) links the two runnable examples and theory derivations. The [solver benchmarks](../docs/studies/validation/spectral-radiation/benchmark.md) report the tested spectral and angular ranges. The [Terra comparison](../docs/studies/validation/spectral-radiation/observations.md) reports separate absolute flux and directional radiance residuals over 32 clear footprints. The full granules remain in the user’s data directory. The repository carries the footprint values, file identities, extraction scripts, and offline replay.

Use the CPU solver for parcel diagnostics and small overpass samples. On the development host, for a 217-node, two-layer column at eight streams per hemisphere and one OpenBLAS thread, a warmed hemispheric flux solve took about 0.014 s and a directional solve over a 5 m/s ocean took about 0.35 s. The observation replay, including all six cases at 32 footprints, took 16.7 s on four CPU workers. A GPU implementation would need a target scene volume, workload-specific profiling, and parity checks against these fixtures.
